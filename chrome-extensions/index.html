About this policy

  • Only the Time Loop extension passes anything to Loop UX: blocks of work you add as entries in Time Loop, and the moment your computer went idle, to stop a running timer.
  • What they read stays on your computer, with 3 exceptions: OTP SmartFill’s requests to Gmail; the codes, and who sent them, that it shows on or fills into the page in front; and the blocks of work the Time Loop extension gives Time Loop’s page in your browser. Besides that, Chrome can sync the settings of Googliam Chatner, René Discards and Later Gator. DNS Organizer fills folder names into a DNS record’s comment, which Simply.com stores when you save the record. Call Light sends checks and on or off commands to the IP address you entered for your Key Light.
  • They are published privately in the Chrome Web Store, so only accounts in Loop UX’s own Google Workspace can install them. “Staff” on this page means the people with a loop-ux.com account, at Loop UX ApS or at Loop UX Inc.
  • This policy covers only the extensions. It adds to Loop UX’s Data Protection Policy and does not replace it. If you take part in a study, the Data Protection Policy is the policy for your data, also when an extension reads it, such as an email you sent to a staff member. Its purposes, legal basis and storage periods apply wherever this page says something different about study data. This page adds what the extensions read, keep and send, who gets the data through them, where it goes and your rights under the GDPR. For staff, and for others who do not take part in a study, this page is the notice for the extensions. The Google Workspace add-ons have their own policy.

Each extension has its own section, and the shared terms at the end apply to all 8. This page does not cover how Loop UX handles staff data in its Google Workspace in general; staff can ask about that at data@loop-ux.com.

OTP SmartFill

OTP SmartFill reads Gmail, read-only, for new emails that look like they hold a code, and shows each code on the page in front. It keeps codes in memory for about 5 minutes and sends nothing to Loop UX.

Reads
New emails in your Gmail that look like they hold a code, read-only.
Keeps
The last 5 codes, in memory, for about 5 minutes. Message IDs and settings.
Sends
Requests to Google’s Gmail API. Each new code and its sender to the page in front. Nothing to Loop UX.

What it reads from Gmail

It reads your Gmail, read-only, only after you agree to its notice and allow Google’s access request. That agreement is not the legal basis; the bases are in the shared terms. It searches your recent mail, including Spam, for emails that look like they hold a code, which can include emails not about signing in. It reads their sender, subject and text on your computer and never sends, changes, deletes or labels email.

What it keeps, and for how long

Everything stays in Chrome on your computer, not synced: the last 5 codes, with each sender’s name, email address and subject, only in memory for about 5 minutes each and none after Chrome closes, and their notifications until a code leaves that list or Chrome next starts; Gmail’s IDs, without content, of the last 50 messages it opened; and its settings. Chrome keeps your Google sign-in, which the extension uses only for Gmail.

What it shows and fills in

Each new code appears with its sender in a card on the page in front and in a system notification. It fills a code in by itself only on what it takes to be the sender’s site or sign-in page, elsewhere only after you click Fill. A filled-in code goes to that site, and scripts it loads can see it; the site in front may be able to see the card. It can copy a code to your clipboard without clearing it, and your operating system may keep codes and notifications in its history.

What it sends

Requests to Google’s Gmail API to read the emails described above; Gmail is part of Loop UX’s Google Workspace, where Google acts as Loop UX’s processor. Apart from what it shows on and fills into pages, nothing goes to Loop UX or anyone else, with no analytics, ads, error reports or tracking. The data is never sold, used for advertising or credit decisions, or passed on except as this section describes.

Limited Use

The use of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

OTP SmartFill’s use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Your choices

Switch off “Check Gmail for codes” to stop it reading Gmail. Removing the extension deletes everything it stored, and you can remove its access in your Google Account.

Googliam Chatner

Googliam Chatner hides some notices and labels in Google Chat and shows flag emoji on Windows. It keeps no page text and makes no network requests.

Reads
On Windows only: the text of the pages you open, to find flag emoji.
Keeps
Your on/off settings for its 4 features.
Sends
Nothing.

What it reads

Hiding items in Google Chat reads nothing. On Windows only, it checks the text of pages you open, including what you type, for flag emoji, but skips password fields and fields marked for one-time codes or card details. It does this in your browser and drops the text right after.

What it keeps

Only your on/off settings for its 4 features, which Chrome can sync through your Google account. They stay until you change them or remove the extension.

What it sends

Nothing, to Loop UX or anyone else. There are no analytics or ads.

René Discards

René Discards hides reply clutter in Gmail, such as quoted earlier emails. It reads the open conversation in your browser, keeps none of it and makes no network requests.

Reads
The open Gmail conversation, in memory.
Keeps
Only its switch settings.
Sends
Nothing.

What it reads

While a Gmail conversation is open, it reads each message’s subject and text, including the sender’s name and email address, in memory and only while it decides what to hide.

What it changes

Only how Gmail looks in your browser, never an email or a draft.

What it keeps

Only its switch settings, which Chrome can sync through your Google account, until you change them or remove the extension.

What it sends

Nothing. It makes no network requests and has no analytics or tracking.

Later Gator

Later Gator hides the early hours in Google Calendar’s day and week views. It never reads your events and sends nothing.

Reads
Calendar’s page address and your scrolling there. Never your events.
Keeps
2 settings: whether hiding is on, and your start hour.
Sends
Nothing.

What it reads

On calendar.google.com only, it reads the page address and your scrolling, never your events, and saves or sends nothing it reads.

What it keeps

2 settings, whether hiding is on and your start hour, which Chrome can sync through your Google account. They stay until you change them or remove the extension.

What it sends

Nothing. It makes no network requests and has no analytics or ads.

Call Light

Call Light turns your Elgato Key Light on and off for Meet and Teams calls. It talks only to the IP address you enter and sends nothing to Loop UX.

Reads
The web addresses of your Meet and Teams tabs.
Keeps
Your light’s IP address; while Chrome is open, which tabs are in a call.
Sends
Checks and on or off, only to the IP address you entered.

What it reads

It checks the addresses of your Google Meet and Microsoft Teams tabs in your browser to tell when you are in a call, and does not save or send them.

What it keeps

Your light’s IP address, on your computer, not synced, and until Chrome closes, which tabs are in a call. Removing the extension deletes both.

Where it connects

Only to the IP address you enter, to check and switch the light. On another network, that address may reach someone else’s light. There are no analytics, error reports or ads.

DNS Organizer

DNS Organizer groups the DNS records in Simply.com’s control panel into folders, which are kept in each record’s own comment. It sends nothing to Loop UX or anyone else.

Reads
The DNS table, a record’s Comment field and the page address.
Keeps
A pending folder change, until it is used or cleaned up.
Sends
Nothing.

What it reads

On the DNS pages of Simply.com’s control panel only, it reads the DNS records with their comments, a record’s Comment field and the page address, in your browser, to sort the records into folders.

What it changes

It fills in only a record’s Comment field, with your note and a folder marker, and never saves the form. Once you select “Update DNS record”, Simply.com stores the comment, and everyone who can view the domain’s DNS there can read it.

What it keeps

On your computer, not synced, it keeps a pending folder change: the domain name, the record’s number and the folder, but no comment or DNS values. It is deleted once used or, if unused, at the latest when you remove the extension. The folders stay in your records’ comments at Simply.com, also after you remove the extension.

What it sends

Nothing. Apart from opening a record’s edit form at Simply.com, it makes no network requests and has no analytics or ads.

Default Zoom

Default Zoom opens Google Docs and Google Sheets at the zoom level you choose. It reads only the zoom control on those pages and sends nothing.

Reads
The zoom control in Docs and Sheets. Never your documents.
Keeps
Its settings: a zoom level for Docs and one for Sheets, and 2 switches.
Sends
Nothing.

What it reads

It runs on Google Docs documents, Google Sheets spreadsheets and Google Classroom pages. There it reads the page address and the zoom control in the toolbar, and uses the zoom menu to set your level. On Google Classroom it does this only if you turn that option on. It never reads the text of a document or a spreadsheet, and it saves or sends nothing it reads.

What it keeps

Its settings: the zoom level for Docs and the one for Sheets, and whether its 2 experimental options, for view-only Docs and for Google Classroom, are on. Chrome can sync them through your Google account. They stay until you change or reset them, or remove the extension.

What it sends

Nothing. It makes no network requests and has no analytics or ads.

Time Loop extension

The Time Loop extension proposes time entries from the sites you worked on. Once you turn it on, it reads your browsing history on your computer and turns it into blocks of work for Time Loop, which reach Loop UX only if you add them.

Reads
Once you turn it on: your browsing history in your working hours, on this computer. Whether the computer is idle.
Keeps
Your settings and the account it answers; until Chrome closes, when the computer went idle.
Sends
Blocks of work and idle times to Time Loop. A block reaches Loop UX only if you add it.

What it reads

It reads the address, title and visit times of the pages you visited on this computer, only in your working hours, with an hour either side, or the hours you choose, at most 14 days back. It never sees incognito windows and never reads mail, chat, Google account, banking or health sites, or sites you or your Google Workspace admin leave out. It also reads whether your computer is idle, and gets your name, email address, working hours and projects from Time Loop.

What it does with it

On your computer, it groups the visits into blocks of work: a day, start and end times, a label from the site and title of the page you spent most time on, up to 3 site names and any project number in a page title. Where Chrome has its built-in AI, Gemini Nano, it suggests a project on your computer, marked as picked by AI. The visits themselves are not saved or sent.

What it keeps

On your computer, not synced: its settings, your working hours and sites to leave out, and your name and email address. Until Chrome closes, it also keeps when your computer went idle. Removing the extension deletes all of it.

What it sends

It answers only Time Loop, and only the person who opened it in this browser, giving it the blocks of work and when your computer went idle. Blocks are suggestions only you see; one reaches Loop UX only when you add it as an entry, with its times and label. If your computer stays idle for the time Loop UX sets while your timer runs, Time Loop stops it at the moment you went idle. It makes no network requests of its own; what Time Loop does with your entries is in Time Loop’s section of the add-ons policy.

Your choices

It is off until you turn it on. You can turn it off, pause it, choose other hours and change the sites to leave out.

Who is responsible, your rights and how to contact Loop UX: Shared terms

Shared terms

These terms apply to all 8 extensions. They add to Loop UX’s Data Protection Policy and do not replace it. If you take part in a study, that policy sets what your data is used for, on what basis and how long it is kept, wherever these terms say something different about study data. The recipients, transfers and rights in these terms add to it.

Who is responsible

Loop UX ApS, Bryghuspladsen 8, 3rd Floor, 1473 Copenhagen K, Denmark, CVR no. 37 82 07 76, provides the extensions to its staff and is the data controller for what they handle, under the GDPR. Write to data@loop-ux.com with any question about this policy.

Purpose and legal basis

The extensions make everyday work at Loop UX quicker: signing in, reading Chat and Gmail, seeing flag emoji on Windows, planning the day, joining calls, keeping DNS records in order and recording working time. For staff, Loop UX relies on its legitimate interest in giving its staff these tools (GDPR Article 6(1)(f)). Some extensions also read, on your computer, what other people wrote, such as the emails in a Gmail conversation, and use it only as their sections describe. For client contacts and other people outside Loop UX who do not take part in a study, Loop UX relies on its legitimate interest in doing its work with these tools (Article 6(1)(f)). For research participants, Loop UX’s Data Protection Policy applies. The effect on privacy is small: apart from the exceptions in About this policy, what the extensions read stays on your computer, and Loop UX receives only the entries you add in Time Loop from the Time Loop extension’s suggestions, and the end time of a timer it stopped. You can object to that; see Your right to object. The extensions make no automated decisions with legal or similarly significant effects on anyone.

Google’s role

OTP SmartFill’s requests go to Gmail, which is part of Loop UX’s Google Workspace: there Google acts as Loop UX’s processor, under its Cloud Data Processing Addendum. Chrome can sync the settings of Googliam Chatner, René Discards and Later Gator through the Google account your Chrome profile syncs with, under the Google terms that apply to that account.

Loop UX’s Google Workspace keeps the stored content of its main services, such as Gmail, in Europe. Google also processes data in the United States. For what the extensions handle, these transfers rely on the European Commission’s decision that U.S. companies certified under the EU–U.S. Data Privacy Framework protect personal data adequately. Google LLC is on the framework’s list as an active participant, for staff data too (checked on Oct 12, 2026). For processing in other countries outside the EU/EEA, Google uses the European Commission’s standard contractual clauses; write to data@loop-ux.com for a copy.

Chrome Web Store User Data Policy

All 8 extensions follow the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you can ask Loop UX what data it holds about you, and ask it to correct or erase that data or to restrict its use. Loop UX receives no data from the extensions except the entries you add in Time Loop from the Time Loop extension’s suggestions and the end time of a timer it stopped, so a request about the others will usually find nothing; Time Loop’s section of the add-ons policy covers these. You can ask about your other data at Loop UX too; if you took part in a study, Loop UX’s Data Protection Policy also applies to that request. Removing an extension deletes what it kept on your computer and, for Googliam Chatner, René Discards and Later Gator, their settings in your Google account if Chrome sync is on. Send requests to data@loop-ux.com. Loop UX answers within 1 month. If a request is complex, or one person sends many, Loop UX can take up to 2 more months and tells you so within the first month. Loop UX may ask you to confirm who you are.

You can complain to the Danish Data Protection Agency (Datatilsynet) or to the authority in the EU/EEA country where you live or work.

No law requires you to use the extensions. Loop UX may ask you to use one for your work; without them, you do the same tasks by hand.

Your right to object

Where Loop UX relies on its legitimate interest, you can object at any time, for reasons that have to do with your situation. Write to data@loop-ux.com. Loop UX then stops, unless it shows compelling legitimate grounds that override your interests, rights and freedoms, or needs the data to establish, exercise or defend legal claims.

Changes

If an extension starts handling data differently, this policy and the store listing are updated before that version is published, with a new date at the top. OTP SmartFill asks you to agree again before it reads Gmail on new terms.
