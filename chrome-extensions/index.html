About this policy

  • None of the extensions sends anything to Loop UX.
  • What they read stays on your computer, with 2 exceptions: OTP Smart Fill’s requests to Gmail, and the codes it fills in for you. Besides that, Chrome can sync the settings of Googliam Chatner, Renee Discards and Later Gator, and Simply DNS Organizer adds folder names to your DNS records’ comments.
  • They are published privately in the Chrome Web Store, so only people with a loop-ux.com Google account can install them. “Staff” on this page means the people with a loop-ux.com account, at Loop UX ApS or at Loop UX Inc.
  • This policy covers only the extensions. Loop UX’s policy for research participants doesn’t apply to them, and the Google Workspace add-ons have their own policy.

Each extension has its own section, which says what it reads, what it keeps and for how long, and what it sends. The shared terms at the end apply to all 6. For staff, Loop UX’s internal staff notice says more about how Loop UX handles staff data in its Google Workspace.

OTP Smart Fill

OTP Smart Fill reads new sign-in emails in your Gmail, read-only, and fills the code into the page you’re on. It keeps codes in Chrome’s memory for about 5 minutes and sends nothing to Loop UX.

Reads
New sign-in emails in your Gmail, read-only.
Keeps
The last 5 codes, in memory, for about 5 minutes. Message IDs and its settings.
Sends
Requests to Google’s Gmail API. Nothing to Loop UX.

What it reads from Gmail

Before it reads anything, the extension’s window shows what it reads, keeps, shows, fills and sends. Only after you agree there, and allow Google’s access request, does it use Google’s Gmail API, with read-only access (the gmail.readonly scope). Only accounts in the Loop UX Google Workspace can sign in. Agreeing there confirms that you have seen what the extension does, as the Chrome Web Store requires. The legal basis stays Loop UX’s legitimate interest, and you can switch the extension off at any time.

While “Check Gmail for codes” is on, it regularly searches your recent mail, including Spam but not Trash, for emails with a sign-in code. It opens each new match once and reads its sender, subject and text on your computer to find the code. It never sends, changes, deletes or labels email.

What it keeps, and for how long

Everything stays on your computer, in Chrome, and isn’t synced:

  • The last 5 codes, each with its sender (name and email address), subject, the time it was found and Gmail’s ID for the message. The extension keeps them in Chrome’s memory and never writes them to disk; they’re gone when Chrome closes. Each is removed about 5 minutes after it arrived, and “Clear” removes them all at once.
  • Gmail’s IDs for the last 50 messages it opened, so it doesn’t open a message twice. These IDs contain no message content and stay until newer ones replace them.
  • Whether “Check Gmail for codes” is on, which version of the notice you agreed to and, until you agree, that checking was paused for it.

Chrome manages the Google sign-in token. The extension holds it in memory and uses it only for requests to the Gmail API.

What it shows and fills in

A new code is shown on the page you have open when it arrives, in a card with your recent codes, and filled into that page’s code field. The extension doesn’t see your tabs’ addresses. It skips fields for other kinds of code, such as a postcode, promo code or card number. A code filled into a page is, as intended, given to that site.

  • Frames from other sites inside the page, such as ads, never get the code.
  • On some sign-in pages it also presses the submit button. Elsewhere it only moves to the button.
  • With no code field on the page, it copies the code to your clipboard, where it stays until you copy something else.
  • It shows no card and fills nothing on mail.google.com.

Each new code also appears in a system notification with the sender’s name. The extension removes it when the code leaves its list or, if Chrome closed first, when it next starts. Your operating system may keep notifications in its own history until you dismiss them.

What it sends

Only requests to Google’s Gmail API (gmail.googleapis.com), over HTTPS, to search for and read the messages described above. Gmail is part of Loop UX’s Google Workspace, where Google acts as Loop UX’s processor. Nothing goes to Loop UX or anyone else, and there are no analytics, ads, error reports or tracking. The extension gives no one at Loop UX access to your email or your codes, and the data is never sold, shared, or used for advertising or credit decisions.

Limited Use

The use of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

OTP Smart Fill’s use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Your choices

  • Switch off “Check Gmail for codes” to stop reading Gmail without signing out.
  • Click “Clear” to remove the recent codes.
  • Remove the extension to delete everything it stored, and remove its access under third-party connections in your Google Account’s security settings.

Googliam Chatner

Googliam Chatner hides “Deleted message” notices, “Edited” labels and space notifications in Google Chat, and shows flag emoji on Windows. It collects nothing and makes no network requests: everything it uses, including its fonts, is part of the extension.

Reads
On Windows only: the text of the pages you open, to find flag emoji.
Keeps
Your on/off settings for its 4 features.
Sends
Nothing.

What it reads

  • Google Chat: nothing. It adds a stylesheet that hides what you chose to hide.
  • Flag emoji, on Windows only: it checks the text of the pages you open for flag characters, including what you type into forms and editors, but never fields for passwords, one-time codes or card details. It does this in your browser, only to apply a font where a flag shows, and drops the text as soon as the check is done.

What it keeps

Only your on/off settings for its 4 features, in Chrome’s sync storage. If Chrome sync is on, Chrome syncs them through your Google account like your other Chrome settings. They stay until you change them or remove the extension.

What it sends

Nothing, to Loop UX or anyone else. There are no analytics or ads.

Renee Discards

Renee Discards hides reply clutter in Gmail, such as the earlier emails quoted under a reply. It reads the open conversation in your browser to decide what is safe to hide, keeps none of it and makes no network requests.

Reads
The open Gmail conversation, in memory.
Keeps
Only its switch settings.
Sends
Nothing.

What it reads

While a Gmail conversation is open, it reads each message’s sender name and email address, the subject and the text of each message. It reads them in memory, on your computer, only so that the forwarded emails it recognizes, and Outlook history from people who haven’t written in the conversation, stay visible. It holds them only while each check runs.

What it changes

It adds a stylesheet that hides the kinds of clutter you switched on. It never changes an email or a draft.

What it keeps

Only its switch settings: the main switch and one for each kind of clutter, in Chrome’s sync storage. If Chrome sync is on, Chrome syncs them with the rest of your profile. They stay until you change them or remove the extension.

What it sends

Nothing. It makes no network requests, loads no remote code and has no analytics or tracking. Nothing it reads leaves your computer.

Later Gator

Later Gator hides the early hours in Google Calendar’s day and week views, so your day starts at the hour you pick. It never reads your events and sends nothing.

Reads
The page address, the time grid’s size and your scrolling there. Never your events.
Keeps
2 settings: whether hiding is on, and your start hour.
Sends
Nothing.

What it reads

On calendar.google.com only, it reads the page address to tell which view is open, measures Calendar’s time grid, and watches your scrolling there to stop at your start hour. It doesn’t read, change or delete your events, and nothing it reads is saved or sent.

What it keeps

2 settings: whether hiding is on, and the hour your day starts. They’re in Chrome’s sync storage; if Chrome sync is on, Chrome syncs them through your Google account like your other Chrome settings. They stay until you change them or remove the extension.

What it sends

Nothing, to Loop UX or anyone else. It makes no network requests, and there are no analytics or ads.

Elgato Meet Light

Elgato Meet Light turns your Elgato Key Light on when you join a Google Meet or Microsoft Teams call in Chrome, and off when you leave. It talks only to your light, on your own network, and sends nothing to Loop UX.

Reads
The web addresses of your Meet and Teams tabs.
Keeps
Your Key Light’s IP address and, while Chrome is open, which tabs are in a call.
Sends
On and off, to your light only.

What it reads

To tell when you’re in a call, it checks the web address of your tabs on meet.google.com, teams.microsoft.com, teams.live.com and teams.cloud.microsoft against the pattern of a call link, such as a Google Meet meeting code. It does this in your browser. It doesn’t save or send these addresses, and it doesn’t read or change the pages.

What it keeps

  • Your Key Light’s IP address, which you enter in the extension’s window, in Chrome’s local extension storage on your computer. It isn’t synced.
  • While Chrome is open, which tabs are in a call, as tab ID numbers only, in Chrome’s session storage. Chrome clears it when it closes.

Removing the extension deletes both.

Where it connects

Only to the IP address you enter, over your local network: to check that the light answers, to read whether it’s on, and to turn it on or off. The only thing it sends is that on or off command. Chrome asks your permission to reach that address when you click Save, and saving a new address gives up access to the old one.

There are no analytics, error reports or ads.

Simply DNS Organizer

Simply DNS Organizer groups the DNS records in Simply.com’s control panel into folders, which are kept in each record’s own comment. It collects nothing and makes no network requests.

Reads
The DNS table, a record’s Comment field and the page address.
Keeps
A pending folder change, deleted once it is used.
Sends
Nothing.

What it reads

On the DNS pages of Simply.com’s control panel only, it reads the table of DNS records (each record’s type, hostname, value and comment), the Comment field of a record’s edit form, and the page address, which names the domain and the record. It reads them in your browser, to draw the folders, the filters and the search, and to add the folder to the comment. Nothing it reads is sent anywhere.

What it changes

On the DNS list it adds folder rows to the table, filters and a search field above it, and a folder button for each record. When you pick a folder for a record, it opens that record’s usual edit form and fills in 1 field, Comment: your note, followed by a folder marker. It never changes a record’s type, hostname, value or TTL, and it never saves the form. Nothing changes at Simply.com until you select “Update DNS record”. Simply.com then stores the comment, with its marker, like any other edit, and everyone who can view the domain’s DNS there can read it.

What it keeps

A pending folder change, while you go from the DNS list to the edit form: the domain name, the record, the folder you chose and the time. It doesn’t keep the comment itself or any DNS values. This is in Chrome’s local extension storage on your computer and isn’t synced. It is deleted when the form is filled in, the next time you open a DNS list, or when you remove the extension.

The folders themselves aren’t kept by the extension. They are in your records’ comments at Simply.com, and they stay there if you remove the extension: edit a comment in Simply.com to take a marker out.

What it sends

Nothing, to Loop UX or anyone else. It makes no network requests, and there are no analytics or ads.

Shared terms

These terms apply to all 6 extensions.

Who is responsible

Loop UX ApS, Bryghuspladsen 8, 3. sal, 1473 København K, Denmark, CVR no. 37 82 07 76, makes the extensions and is the data controller for them under the GDPR. Write to data@loop-ux.com with any question about this policy.

Purpose and legal basis

The extensions make everyday work at Loop UX quicker: signing in, reading Chat and Gmail, planning the day, joining calls and keeping DNS records in order. Loop UX relies on its legitimate interest in giving its staff these tools (GDPR Article 6(1)(f)). The effect on your privacy is small: what the extensions read stays on your computer, and Loop UX never receives it. You can object to that; see Your right to object. The extensions make no automated decisions about anyone.

Google’s role

OTP Smart Fill’s requests go to Gmail, which is part of Loop UX’s Google Workspace: there Google acts as Loop UX’s processor, under its Cloud Data Processing Addendum. Chrome can sync the settings of Googliam Chatner, Renee Discards and Later Gator through your Loop UX Google account, under the Google terms that apply to that account.

Loop UX has set its Google Workspace to keep the stored content of its main services, such as Gmail, in Europe. Google also processes data in the United States. The European Commission has decided that U.S. companies certified under the EU–U.S. Data Privacy Framework protect personal data adequately, and Google LLC is on the framework’s list as an active participant (checked on Oct 1, 2026). These transfers rely on that decision. For processing in other countries outside the EU/EEA, Google uses the European Commission’s standard contractual clauses; write to data@loop-ux.com for a copy. Loop UX itself transfers no data from the extensions to anyone, inside or outside the EU/EEA.

Chrome Web Store User Data Policy

All 6 extensions follow the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you can ask Loop UX what data it holds about you, have it corrected or erased, and have its use restricted. Loop UX receives no data from the extensions, so a request about them will usually find nothing; you can ask about your other data at Loop UX too. Removing an extension deletes what it kept on your computer, and OTP Smart Fill’s codes can also be cleared in its window. Send requests to data@loop-ux.com. Loop UX answers within 1 month, free of charge, and may ask you to confirm who you are.

You can complain to the Danish Data Protection Agency (Datatilsynet) or to the authority in the EU/EEA country where you live or work.

You don’t have to install the extensions. Without them, you do the same tasks by hand.

Your right to object

Loop UX relies on its legitimate interest. You can object to that at any time, for reasons that have to do with your situation. Write to data@loop-ux.com. Loop UX then stops, unless it has compelling reasons to continue.

Changes

If an extension starts handling data differently, this policy and the store listing are updated before that version is published, with a new date at the top. Loop UX tells its staff about changes that matter before they take effect, and OTP Smart Fill asks you to agree again before it reads Gmail on new terms.
