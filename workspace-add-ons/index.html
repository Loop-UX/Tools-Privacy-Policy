About this policy

  • The add-ons run in Loop UX’s own Google Workspace and Google Cloud. What they read stays there, except what they pass on. Emails and calendar invitations go to the people you address, and Loopy’s automatic replies go to the people who wrote. 3 add-ons also send some of what they handle to another company: Scheduler sends meeting titles, times and hosts to Zoom, GWS Whizard’s “Send invoice to Corpay” emails a document to Corpay, and peazySign sends contracts and the signers’ names and email addresses to BoldSign, an e-signature service.
  • Time Loop, Loop UX’s time tracking web app, is on this page too. Freelancers Loop UX invites sign in to it with the account Loop UX gives them.
  • The add-ons are published privately in the Google Workspace Marketplace, so only accounts in Loop UX’s own Google Workspace can install them. “Staff” on this page means the people with a loop-ux.com account, at Loop UX ApS or at Loop UX Inc.
  • Loop UX does not sell what the add-ons handle, use it for advertising or use it to train AI models.
  • This policy covers only the add-ons. It adds to Loop UX’s Data Protection Policy and does not replace it. If you take part in a study, the Data Protection Policy is the policy for your data, also when staff use an add-on with it: its purposes, legal basis and storage periods apply wherever this page says something different about study data. This page adds what the add-ons read, keep and send, who gets the data through them, where it goes and your rights under the GDPR. For staff, and for clients, freelancers and others who do not take part in a study, this page is the notice for the add-ons. The Chrome extensions have their own policy.

Each add-on has its own section. Some add-ons also handle data about people who do not work at Loop UX: People outside Loop UX says what that means for them. The shared terms at the end apply to all the add-ons. This page does not cover how Loop UX handles staff data in its Google Workspace in general; staff can ask about that at data@loop-ux.com.

Loopy

Loopy handles Gmail aliases, mail merges from Google Sheets, Workspace Studio file steps, Google Chat time zones and automatic replies for closed studies. Apart from the emails it sends, everything stays in Loop UX’s Google Workspace and Google Cloud.

Reads
Your aliases, the sheet and draft you merge, file names in a flow, your Chat space’s members and their time zones, and emails to closed study addresses.
Keeps
Your Chat settings, Chat members’ names, email addresses and time zones, and names and places added with a time zone, until replaced or removed.
Sends
The emails you send with it and its automatic replies. Nothing to companies other than Google.

Gmail: email aliases

Loopy reads your primary address and your aliases from Loop UX’s Google Workspace directory, and no email. An alias you add stays until it is removed.

Google Sheets: mail merge

Loopy reads the whole open tab, the subjects of the drafts in the mailbox you send from and the draft you pick. It sends 1 email per row from your own or a shared Loop UX mailbox, where the emails stay in Sent. Sent as replies, they can also reach others on each address’s latest thread.

Workspace Studio: flow steps

A step reads the name of a Drive file and of its folder, never what is in the file, and renames the file or labels it as confidential. It passes the names and links on to the next steps; the flow’s activity shows the file’s name and link.

Google Chat: time zones

For /tz, Loopy reads who is in the space and, for each Loop UX colleague, whether or not they use Loopy, their name and email address from the directory and their primary calendar’s time zone, never its events. Loopy keeps the members’ details until a new lookup replaces them, a refresh deletes them or Loopy leaves the space; any member can ask to see their names and time zones. People outside Loop UX are not in the directory, so Loopy keeps their name and time zone only if someone adds them by hand; names added in a space stay until Loopy leaves it, and names or places pinned for all colleagues until someone removes them. The answer everyone sees shows regions and times, no names, and Loopy does not use or keep other messages.

Research inbox: automatic replies

When someone writes to a closed study’s address and no one at Loop UX has answered, Loopy sends 1 automatic reply from a shared Loop UX mailbox, where it stays. To reply in the sender’s language, the email’s text, or its beginning, goes to Google Cloud Translation, and for senders not writing in English so does the reply, with their first name. If the sender took part in a study, Loop UX’s Data Protection Policy applies to their data.

What it sends

Nothing to companies other than Google. Mail merge emails and automatic replies go to their recipients through Gmail.

Your choices

You can remove an alias in Gmail with Loopy, and delete members’ details in a space with /tz-config refresh or by removing Loopy. A time zone you set with /tz-config stays until you change it and applies only to your own /tz.

GWS Whizard

GWS Whizard adds formatting tools for the open file in Google Docs, Sheets and Slides. Only “Send invoice to Corpay” sends anything out: after you confirm, it emails the document to Corpay.

Reads
The file that is open.
Keeps
No copies of your files. The email “Send invoice to Corpay” sends stays in your Gmail.
Sends
Only “Send invoice to Corpay”, after you confirm: the document as a PDF, by email.

Docs

The tools change the open document in place and open no other. 2 tools format transcripts from transcription services with the speaker names you type.

Send invoice to Corpay

After you confirm, this tool emails the open document as a PDF from your Gmail to Corpay, the provider Loop UX pays invoices through, with its file name and your name and email address. Corpay One handles the invoice for Loop UX; see Who gets the data. The sent email then goes to your Gmail Trash, where Gmail deletes it after 30 days.

Sheets

The tools change the selected cells or, for some, the whole sheet or every visible tab, and open no other spreadsheet. “Tab Sync” copies details, such as names and contact details, between tabs of the same spreadsheet; nothing leaves the spreadsheet.

Slides

The tools change the tables, elements and text of the open presentation and open no other.

What it keeps

No copies of your files: changes stay in the file. The one exception is the email from Send invoice to Corpay: it goes to your Gmail Trash, where Gmail deletes it after 30 days, or stays in your Sent mail or Drafts if moving or sending it fails. GWS Whizard also writes technical logs; see How long data is kept.

What it sends

The email to Corpay. Nothing else goes to any company other than Google.

Bloodgethound

Bloodgethound adds Loop UX’s project tools, such as the fieldwork Schedule, to Google Sheets. It works in the open spreadsheet and sends nothing from it outside Google.

Reads
The open spreadsheet: its schedule, invoice rows, tab names and file name.
Keeps
The schedule, in the spreadsheet itself. Place searches, for a few hours.
Sends
Place names you search, to Google. The Holiday Calendar shows a page from officeholidays.com.

The Schedule

The Schedule keeps fieldwork sessions in the spreadsheet, with a free-text session field that can hold a participant’s code or name. You can copy them for Loop UX’s planning spreadsheet, where staff use codes, not names, and Scheduler books them in Zoom and Google Calendar with that text in their titles. Its tabs, which show session names, can be shared with the project’s client.

Other tools

The invoice and row tools change rows of the open sheet, and the custom formulas read tab and file names. In some sheets, the add-on stamps the time of an edit in a set range, without reading what changed or noting who edited.

What it keeps

The schedule, the Schedule tabs and the timestamps stay in the spreadsheet as long as it exists; see How long data is kept. Place searches are kept for a few hours.

What it sends

Place names you search go to Google’s geocoder; the add-on sends nothing from the spreadsheet to anyone else. “Holiday Calendar” shows officeholidays.com, which, with other companies it loads, such as advertising services, sees your IP address and browser details and may set cookies.

Adobe-wan Kenobi

Adobe-wan Kenobi (PDF Whizard) splits and merges PDFs and converts images and SVGs in Google Drive, through Loop UX’s own service on Google Cloud in the EU, which keeps no copy.

Reads
The files you select in Drive, and whether they are labeled confidential.
Keeps
The new files, in Drive. No copy on the service.
Sends
The selected files, to Loop UX’s own service on Google Cloud in the EU.

What it reads

It reads the files you select in Drive, their folders and confidential labels, and no other file. It does not use your name or email address.

What it changes

New files go into the original’s folder, or your My Drive, and get its confidential label where the add-on can set it. If you choose, the originals you can delete go to Drive’s trash, where you can restore them for up to 30 days.

The service on Google Cloud

The files’ content and, for some tasks, their names go to Loop UX’s service on Google Cloud in the EU, without your name, email address or access to your Google account. It keeps no copy after the request.

What it keeps

The new files stay in Drive under the rules in How long data is kept; the add-on stores nothing else apart from technical logs.

What it sends

Only the requests to that service. Nothing goes to Adobe or to any company other than Google.

Scheduler

Scheduler adds Zoom to Google Calendar events, imports Zoom transcripts as Google Docs, and turns sessions in Loop UX’s planning spreadsheet into Zoom meetings and calendar invitations. Meeting titles, times and hosts go to Zoom, through Loop UX’s account.

Reads
Events you add Zoom to, the planning spreadsheet, the calendars it is set up to read, Loop UX’s Zoom users, meetings and recordings, and imported transcripts.
Keeps
Your meeting details until 30 days after the event, with a few exceptions. Shared calendar events until 60 days after they end, or longer while a booking uses them. Zoom meetings and imported transcripts.
Sends
Meeting titles, times and hosts, to Zoom.

Google Calendar

When you add Zoom to an event, Scheduler creates a Zoom meeting from the event’s title and time, not its guests or description, for the host you pick in Loop UX’s Zoom account. It adds the join details and the host’s email address to the event, where its guests see them.

Google Drive: transcripts

Scheduler reads the names and email addresses of Loop UX’s Zoom users and a host’s recent recordings that have a transcript. It imports a transcript, with the speakers’ names and everything that was said, into a new Google Doc or tab where you choose.

The planning spreadsheet

Scheduler turns rows of the planning spreadsheet, with each session’s host, time and guests’ email addresses, into Zoom meetings or webinars and events in a shared Loop UX calendar; staff write a code for the session, not a participant’s name. Google Calendar emails the guests, who can see each other’s email addresses. Its overview reads the calendars it is set up to read, which can include colleagues’ calendars, and Loop UX’s Zoom meetings, and shows each host’s project per day, not the events’ titles or guests.

What it keeps

Scheduler keeps the details of each event you add Zoom to and of its meeting until 30 days after the event ends, with a few exceptions, such as a repeating series, whose details it does not delete by itself. The planning spreadsheet keeps each booking, with its guests’ email addresses, until after the session, at the latest 60 days after the last session that shares its Zoom link, and a booking it cannot check until someone settles it; it also holds the Zoom users’ names and email addresses and a short list of Scheduler’s latest actions and errors. For you, Scheduler keeps the Zoom host you picked last until you pick another, notes of your recent imports until a later import replaces them, and, for a short time, the Zoom users and recordings it lists. Events in the shared calendar and in a second one used for recruitment, with their guests’ email addresses, stay until 60 days after they end, or longer while a booking uses them; Scheduler then deletes them without emailing the guests; copies in guests’ other calendar apps stay. Scheduler deletes a Zoom meeting, with its title, when you delete an event that you organize or every session that uses it is canceled, not when the event ends; it does not delete imported transcripts, which Loop UX keeps as How long data is kept says.

What it sends

Scheduler sends Zoom each meeting’s title, time, host and settings and the email addresses of its other hosts; titles from the planning spreadsheet can hold the project number, the market and the session text. Zoom does not get an event’s guests, its invitation text or any file from Drive.

peazySign

peazySign sends the open tab of a Google Doc to BoldSign, an e-signature service, as a contract, and can save completed BoldSign documents, including responses to Bulk Links, as signed PDFs in a Google Drive folder you choose.

Reads
The open document, Loop UX’s directory and the signers of your recent contracts. For Drive sync: completed BoldSign documents, which can include the sender’s and signers’ names and email addresses.
Keeps
Your connections to BoldSign and Google, Drive sync rules, records of saved documents for 60 days and consultant suggestions for a short time. No copy of a contract or of a signed PDF.
Sends
The contract PDF and signer and CC details, to BoldSign. Drive sync saves signed PDFs in the Drive folder you choose.

What it reads

peazySign reads the name and email address of the BoldSign account you connect, and the names, email addresses and photos of the people in Loop UX’s directory, for the signer and CC lists. When you send a contract, it reads the open document and makes a PDF of the open tab only; to suggest consultants, it uses only the signers’ names and email addresses of documents you sent recently in BoldSign with the tag “Contract”. Loop UX’s Drive sync service reads the folder you choose, not the other files in it, and the details of each document completed in Loop UX’s BoldSign account, including responses to Bulk Links, which can contain the sender’s and signers’ names and email addresses; it saves those that match a rule and that the rule owner’s BoldSign user can read.

What it changes

In Docs, peazySign changes only the tab you send, to prepare it for the PDF, and does not save the PDF in Drive. Drive sync adds signed PDFs to the folder chosen in a rule.

What it keeps

peazySign keeps your BoldSign and Google connections, with their accounts’ names or email addresses, until you disconnect or it finds a connection ended, and, while you connect, briefly your Google account’s identifier and email address; Drive sync keeps your Google account’s identifier until your Loop UX account is closed. For a short time, it keeps the names and email addresses of the signers outside Loop UX’s directory that it suggests only to you, and it keeps a list of Loop UX accounts left out of the signer lists until the list changes. Drive sync keeps each rule, with its owner’s account, until someone removes it or 90 days after the owner’s Loop UX account is closed, unless a colleague takes it over, and a record of each completed document for 30 days and of each saved document, with the rule owner’s account, for 60 days after the last attempt to save it. It keeps neither BoldSign’s notice nor the document’s details, and peazySign keeps no copy of a contract or of a signed PDF.

What it sends

peazySign sends BoldSign, as your connected BoldSign user, the open tab as a PDF, the names and email addresses of the consultant and of the colleague who signs for Loop UX, the email address of the colleague in CC and the contract’s details, but no other file or colleague. BoldSign keeps a record of the signing with the name, email address, IP address and device of everyone who takes a step, how each signer signed and when. When you open BoldSign’s pages, Syncfusion, Inc., which runs BoldSign, also handles technical data about your device and browser on its own responsibility, to prevent fraud (see Who gets the data), and the page that confirms your connection loads a font and an icon from Google. Drive sync saves signed PDFs in the rule’s folder, where anyone with access, including people outside Loop UX if the folder is shared with them, can read them, and a file name can end with a signer’s name.

How long data is kept

peazySign itself deletes no signed PDF, not even when a rule is removed. Loop UX has set its BoldSign account, which holds the contract, the signers’ names and email addresses and the record of the signing, to delete a document 60 days after it is signed by everyone, declined, canceled by the sender or expired; BoldSign says it keeps backups for 30 days, and it may keep its record of the signing longer than the document, as its terms and the law allow. Loop UX deletes a signed contract from Drive in January, 5 years after the year it was signed, and keeps a signed study agreement as its Data Protection Policy says. BoldSign emails everyone who signed and the colleague in CC the signed contract or a link to it, and the email can include its record of the signing; copies that signers and other recipients receive or download stay with them when Loop UX deletes its own.

Time Loop

Time Loop is Loop UX’s time tracking web app for staff and invited freelancers, in Loop UX’s Google Cloud in the EU. For staff in Denmark it is the record of daily working time that Danish law requires.

Reads
Your Google sign-in and photo; only if you allow it, your calendar and the names and times of files you edit.
Keeps
Your entries and time off, with their history, for 5 years, and your schedule and settings.
Sends
Quick log text, with your tasks and recent entries, to Gemini on Google Cloud; notifications to a Chat space you add. Nothing to companies other than Google.

What it records

For each entry, Time Loop records the day, times, project, task and a note, with a history of who changed what and when. It also keeps your time off, schedule, expected hours, utilization goal, office and lunch days and settings, and you see any correction an admin makes and its reason.

What it reads from Google

Time Loop reads your Google name and email address at sign-in and copies your directory photo. Only if you allow it, it reads your calendar to suggest entries, keeping only office or home for each day and the IDs of meetings you link. Only if you turn them on, it reads which Docs, Sheets and Slides files you edit and when, never their contents, and blocks of work from the Time Loop Chrome extension (see its section). Only you see these suggestions until you add them as entries.

Quick log and AI

Quick log sends what you write to Gemini on Google Cloud in the EU, with your project and task names, working hours and the times and tasks of your last 7 days; under Google Cloud’s terms, Google does not use these requests for training. Spoken words become text on your computer; no audio is sent. AI proposals, from Gemini or Gemini Nano on your computer, are marked as AI, shown only to you and added only if you add them. The AI does not rate, rank or evaluate anyone, its proposals are not used in Insights or elsewhere, meeting titles are not sent to any AI, and Time Loop’s suggestions and reminders never decide anything about you.

Working-time rules

Time Loop checks your hours against the working-time rules where you work, such as rest periods, breaks, night work and US overtime. It reminds you, and your manager and admins see the reminders of the people they look after; reminders never stop you recording the hours you worked.

Who sees your hours

You see your own hours, and freelancers see only their own hours and the parts of projects they work on. By default, your manager sees the hours, time off and reminders of the people in their region, and admins see everyone’s, plus Insights with each person’s hours balance, vacation and utilization; admin notices, such as who has days not recorded, can also go to an admins’ Google Chat space. Only admins can change what each role sees, for example letting managers see everyone or Insights, or colleagues see their project team’s week. Whatever the settings, only you and admins see that a day off was a sick day, and only you see your suggestions and AI proposals.

How long data is kept

Entries, time off and their history are deleted automatically after 5 years; totals per project and month, without names, can be kept longer. Your photo is kept until you leave Loop UX, Quick log requests and answers for 30 days, and a deleted record can stay in backups for up to 12 months.

Your choices

You can connect or disconnect your calendar, turn file and extension suggestions on or off, and choose how Time Loop notifies you; everyone in a Google Chat space you add sees the notifications. Recording your working time is part of your work and, in Denmark, required by law.

People outside Loop UX

Some add-ons handle data about people outside Loop UX. This is what happens with it.

If you take part in a study

Loop UX staff can:

  • email you from a spreadsheet with your name, email address and session details (Loopy);
  • share your session’s schedule, under a code or your name, with the study’s client (Bloodgethound);
  • create your session in Zoom and Google Calendar; Zoom and the guests, such as observers and the client, get its code and time (Scheduler);
  • put a recorded session’s Zoom transcript, with your Zoom name and what you said (which can be sensitive), in a Google Doc (Scheduler);
  • format your session’s transcript from a transcription service in Google Docs, with a name or code per speaker (GWS Whizard);
  • merge, split or convert a document about you on Loop UX’s own service on Google Cloud in the EU, which keeps no copy (Adobe-wan Kenobi);
  • save your signed study agreement to Google Drive (peazySign; see If you sign in BoldSign).

If you email a closed study’s address, you get 1 automatic reply unless someone already answered; to write it in your language, Google Cloud Translation gets your email’s text (or its start) and, unless you write in English, your first name.

Loop UX’s Data Protection Policy applies to your data in a study, also when staff use these add-ons with it. It sets what your data is used for, on what basis and how long it is kept, wherever this page says something different, and it says how to withdraw your consent. This page adds what the add-ons do with your data, who gets it through them, where it goes and your rights under the GDPR.

If you work with Loop UX

This is for clients, freelancers and guests. If you use Time Loop as a freelancer, it keeps your recorded and contract hours. A colleague can add your name and time zone from a shared Google Chat space to Loopy. A colleague’s session invitation puts your email address in Loop UX’s planning spreadsheet (Scheduler) and the Google Calendar event, where other guests see it. Scheduler deletes that event, and those of a shared Loop UX recruitment calendar, 60 days after they end (later while a booking uses them), without emailing you; events these calendars organized then also disappear from your Google Calendar, while a copy in another calendar app stays.

A recorded Zoom meeting you join can leave its transcript, with your name and what you said, in a Google Doc at Loop UX. Loop UX can email your invoice, with what it says about you, such as your bank details, to Corpay One, which pays it for Loop UX (GWS Whizard). If a colleague sent you a BoldSign document tagged “Contract”, as every peazySign contract is, peazySign can suggest your name and email address to them for another contract, even if you did not sign, while BoldSign lists it as recent; peazySign keeps them only briefly. For signing, see If you sign in BoldSign.

If you sign in BoldSign

BoldSign, Loop UX’s e-signature service, gets each contract or study agreement Loop UX sends you there, with your name and email address (for contracts from Google Docs, see peazySign). It keeps the document and a signing record (your name, email and IP address, device, how you signed and the time of each step) in Loop UX’s account in the Netherlands. Syncfusion, Inc., which runs BoldSign, also handles technical data about your device and browser on its pages, on its own responsibility, to prevent fraud (BoldSign’s privacy notice).

Loop UX’s account deletes a document 60 days after it is signed by everyone, declined, canceled by the sender or expired; BoldSign may keep the record longer, as its terms and the law allow. peazySign can also save completed documents to a Google Drive folder, which anyone it is shared with can read, also outside Loop UX. Loop UX deletes a signed contract from Drive in January, 5 years after the year it was signed; a study agreement is kept as the Data Protection Policy says. BoldSign emails all signers and any Loop UX colleague in CC the signed document or a link to it, and the email can include the record; copies people receive or download stay with them (How long data is kept).

Where your data comes from

Your details come from you or your Loop UX contact; for a study, the Data Protection Policy names any other sources. Giving them is optional, but Loop UX needs your email address to invite you or send you a document to sign, and your invoice to pay you.

Your rights

In a study, you can withdraw your consent at any time, as the Data Protection Policy says, without affecting earlier use. Where the basis is Loop UX’s legitimate interest, you can object. The shared terms list your other rights, such as to see, correct or erase your data, and the bases, transfers outside the EU, retention and how to complain. Write to data@loop-ux.com.

Purpose and legal basis

Time Loop records working time, time off and project hours. The add-ons take routine steps out of Loop UX’s project work: email aliases, mailings, automatic replies, time zones in Chat, schedules, video meetings, transcripts, invoices, contracts, filing completed signed documents, and renaming, labeling, splitting, merging, converting and formatting files.

  • Staff: Loop UX’s legitimate interest in doing this work with tools of its own (GDPR Article 6(1)(f)).
  • Time Loop, for staff: for staff employed in Denmark, Loop UX’s legal obligation to record daily working time under the Danish Working Hours Act (Article 6(1)(c)); for pay, expected hours, time off and lunch at the office, your employment contract (Article 6(1)(b)); for project hours, budgets, reminders about rest and working time, and suggestions, Loop UX’s legitimate interest in running its projects and looking after working time (Article 6(1)(f)). A sick day is health data: Loop UX records it only to meet its obligations as an employer under employment law (Article 9(2)(b)).
  • Time Loop, for freelancers: the contract with you for the hours you work for Loop UX (Article 6(1)(b)).
  • Study participants: the basis that Loop UX’s Data Protection Policy names: your consent to the study.
  • Client contacts, freelancers and other people in meetings, invitations, emails and Chat spaces who do not take part in a study: Loop UX’s legitimate interest in planning and documenting its projects (Article 6(1)(f)).
  • People who write to a closed study’s address: if you took part in the study, the basis that Loop UX’s Data Protection Policy names; if you did not, Loop UX’s legitimate interest in answering you (Article 6(1)(f)).
  • People whose invoices Loop UX pays: the contract with you (Article 6(1)(b)) or, if you invoice for a company, Loop UX’s legitimate interest in paying that company (Article 6(1)(f)), and Danish bookkeeping law for keeping the invoice (Article 6(1)(c)).
  • People who sign a contract with Loop UX: the contract with you, and the steps to enter into it (Article 6(1)(b)), or, if you sign for a company, Loop UX’s legitimate interest in entering into the contract with that company (Article 6(1)(f)). For the record of the signing and for keeping the signed contract: Loop UX’s legitimate interest in being able to show who signed, when, and what was agreed (Article 6(1)(f)). For suggesting, when a colleague prepares a contract, the names and email addresses of people that colleague sent earlier contracts to: Loop UX’s legitimate interest in preparing its contracts quickly and correctly (Article 6(1)(f)). This does not cover the agreement for a study you take part in: for that, the basis is the one Loop UX’s Data Protection Policy names.

How long data is kept

Each add-on’s section says how long it keeps what it stores itself. Loop UX keeps the files, emails and calendar events that the add-ons create in Google Workspace under the same rules as the rest of the project or contract they belong to, unless an add-on’s section gives a shorter time:

  • Study material, such as mailing lists, schedules, transcripts and signed study agreements: as Loop UX’s Data Protection Policy says.
  • Other project material, such as discussion guides, reports, meeting transcripts and project emails: Loop UX deletes it when it closes the project.
  • Invoices sent to Corpay: Corpay One’s terms let it keep them while Loop UX uses its service and for up to 24 months after, and keep data longer where the law requires it.
