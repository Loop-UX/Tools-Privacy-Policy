About this policy

  • The add-ons run in Loop UX’s own Google Workspace and Google Cloud. What they read stays there, except what they pass on: emails and calendar invitations go to the people you address, Loopy’s automatic replies go to the people who wrote, and some of it goes to Zoom, Corpay or BoldSign, as the next point says.
  • 3 add-ons send some of what they handle to another company: Scheduler sends meeting titles, times and hosts to Zoom, GWS Whizard’s “Send invoice to Corpay” emails a document to Corpay, and peazySign sends contracts and the signers’ names and email addresses to BoldSign, an e-signature service.
  • Time Loop, Loop UX’s time tracking web app, is on this page too: it opens from the Google app launcher and is listed privately in the Google Workspace Marketplace like the add-ons. Freelancers Loop UX invites sign in to it with the account Loop UX gives them.
  • They are published privately in the Google Workspace Marketplace, so only accounts in Loop UX’s own Google Workspace can install them. “Staff” on this page means the people with a loop-ux.com account, at Loop UX ApS or at Loop UX Inc.
  • Loop UX does not sell what the add-ons handle, use it for advertising or use it to train AI models.
  • This policy covers only the add-ons. It adds to Loop UX’s Data Protection Policy and does not replace it. If you take part in a study, the Data Protection Policy is the policy for your data, also when staff use an add-on with it: its purposes, legal basis and storage periods apply wherever this page says something different about study data. This page adds what the add-ons read, keep and send, who gets the data through them, where it goes and your rights under the GDPR. For staff, and for clients, freelancers and others who do not take part in a study, this page is the notice for the add-ons. The Chrome extensions have their own policy.

Each add-on has its own section, which says what it reads, what it keeps and for how long, and what it sends. Some add-ons also handle data about people who do not work at Loop UX: People outside Loop UX says what that means for them. The shared terms at the end apply to all the add-ons. This page does not cover how Loop UX handles staff data in its Google Workspace in general; staff can ask about that at data@loop-ux.com.

Loopy

Loopy manages your email aliases in Gmail, sends a mail merge from Google Sheets, renames and labels files in Workspace Studio flows, and converts times for your team in Google Chat. It also answers emails to closed study addresses automatically. Apart from the emails it sends, everything it handles stays in Loop UX’s Google Workspace and Google Cloud.

Reads
Your aliases, the sheet and draft you merge, file names in a flow, the members of your Chat space with their names, email addresses and time zones, and emails to closed study addresses.
Keeps
A space’s members and their time zones, until a new lookup replaces them, a refresh deletes them or Loopy leaves the space. Your Chat settings. Pinned places and names, until someone removes them. Names added in a space, until Loopy leaves it.
Sends
The emails you send with it and its automatic replies. Nothing to companies other than Google.

Gmail: email aliases

Opening Loopy in Gmail reads your primary address and the aliases on your account from Loop UX’s Google Workspace directory. It reads no email. An alias you add stays on your account until it is removed. If the alias you ask for is taken, Loopy shows you which colleague has it.

Google Sheets: mail merge

Loopy reads the whole tab that is open, because every column of a row can go into that row’s email. You choose the mailbox to send from: a shared Loop UX mailbox or your own.

  • Loopy reads the subjects of the drafts in the mailbox you send from, so you can pick one.
  • From the draft you pick, it reads the subject, the text, the attachments and the inline images.
  • In “Reply (to all)” mode it looks, in the mailbox you send from, for the latest email to or from each address and answers in that thread, or sends a new email if there is none. From the shared mailbox, the reply goes to the person in the row and to whoever was copied on that email. From your own mailbox, it goes to everyone on the last email of that thread.

“Send all” sends 1 email to the address in each row. If the tab has a column headed Sent, Loopy skips rows with an entry in it, and writes in it the time each email went out, or the error if it failed. The emails stay in the Sent folder of the mailbox, like any other email. “Send preview” sends the first row to the mailbox you send from.

Workspace Studio: flow steps

A flow gives each step the link to a Drive file, and “Rename a file” also gets the new name. The step reads the file’s name and the name and link of its folder, never what is in the file. It renames the file or applies the Drive label that marks a file as confidential, and passes the names and links of the file and its folder on to the steps that follow. Workspace Studio shows the file’s name and link in the flow’s activity.

Google Chat: time zones

/tz converts a time for everyone in a space. For that, Loopy reads who is in the space, asks Loop UX’s directory about each member (it uses the name and email address) and reads the time zone of each Loop UX colleague’s primary Google Calendar, and none of its events. This covers every Loop UX colleague in the space, whether or not they use Loopy. People outside Loop UX are not found in the directory, so Loopy does not keep their names, email addresses or time zones unless someone adds their name and time zone by hand.

Loopy keeps:

  • The names, email addresses and time zones of a space’s members. Loopy uses them for a few hours and then looks them up again at the next /tz. They stay stored until a new lookup replaces them, someone types /tz-config refresh in the space, or Loopy is removed from it. A member of the space can ask Loopy to show the names and time zones it holds for that space.
  • Your settings: 12-hour or 24-hour times, and a time zone you set yourself. They stay until you change them.
  • The places or names with a time zone that the team pinned, and the names with a time zone that someone added in a space. Pinned entries are shared by all spaces, any colleague can list them, and they stay until someone removes them. Names added in a space stay until Loopy is removed from it.

The result of /tz is posted for everyone in the space and shows regions and times, no names. Loopy’s other answers are visible only to you, apart from its welcome message when it joins a space and a short notice when something goes wrong. Loopy acts only on the commands you send it; if you mention it or write to it directly, it does not use or keep the text.

Research inbox: automatic replies

Loopy also answers automatically. When someone writes to a study’s contact address after the study has closed, and no one at Loop UX has answered that email yet, Loopy sends 1 reply in the same thread from a shared Loop UX mailbox. To write it in the sender’s language, it sends the text of the email, or the beginning of a long one, to Google Cloud Translation. For a sender who does not write in English, the reply’s text, with the sender’s first name in the greeting, also goes to Cloud Translation.

Loopy keeps no copy of its own: the reply stays in the mailbox. If the sender took part in a study, Loop UX’s Data Protection Policy applies to their data.

What it sends

Nothing to companies other than Google. Loopy’s requests go only to Google’s own services, such as the Workspace directory, Gmail, Calendar, Chat, Drive, Sheets and Cloud Translation. The emails of a mail merge and the automatic replies go to their recipients through Gmail.

Your choices

  • Remove an alias with its trash icon in Gmail.
  • Type /tz-config refresh in a space to delete the names, email addresses and time zones Loopy looked up for its members; the next /tz looks them up again. Removing Loopy from the space deletes them, and the names added in that space, too.
  • Set your time zone yourself with /tz-config, so your own /tz uses that one instead of your calendar’s. When a colleague uses /tz in a space you are in, Loopy still uses your calendar’s time zone.

GWS Whizard

GWS Whizard adds a menu of formatting tools to Google Docs, Sheets and Slides. Each tool works on the file that is open. 1 tool sends something out: “Send invoice to Corpay” emails the open document to Corpay after you confirm, then moves that email to your Gmail Trash, where Gmail deletes it after 30 days.

Reads
The file that is open, when you run a tool.
Keeps
No settings. The email “Send invoice to Corpay” sends stays in your Gmail Trash for up to 30 days.
Sends
Only “Send invoice to Corpay”, after you confirm: the document as a PDF, by email.

Docs

The tools read the text of the open document and its file name, and change the document in place.

  • 2 tools format transcripts exported from transcription services: they ask you what to call each speaker that has a generic label, such as “Speaker 1”, and write what you type into the document. Nothing goes to those services.
  • “Link timecodes” asks for the link to a recording in Drive and uses it only to build the links.
  • “Set font to Figtree” changes formatting only. “Remove contract’s annotations” deletes the drawings and images in the open tab. It uses the Docs API, so GWS Whizard asks for access to your Google Docs documents; it opens only the document that is open.

Send invoice to Corpay

This tool first asks you to confirm. Nothing is sent until you click “Send invoice”. It then turns the open document into a PDF and emails it from your Gmail account to Corpay, the provider Loop UX pays invoices through. Corpay receives the PDF, the document’s file name as the subject, and your name and email address as the sender. The PDF is not saved in Drive. Corpay One handles the invoice for Loop UX; see Who gets the data.

Afterwards the tool moves the email it sent, with the PDF, to your Gmail Trash. You can restore it from there; after 30 days Gmail deletes it. If the email cannot be moved to the Trash, it stays in your Sent mail, and the tool tells you so. If sending fails after the email was prepared, the unsent email can stay in your Drafts. The invoice document itself stays in Drive. Nothing else in your mail is touched.

This tool is why GWS Whizard asks for access to your Google Drive and your Gmail in Docs. No other tool uses either.

Sheets

The tools read the cells you selected or, for some, the whole sheet or every visible tab, and change them there: formatting, merges, notes, checkboxes and, for some tools, the text in the cells. “Copy content to note” puts each cell’s text into its note. GWS Whizard asks for access to your Google Sheets spreadsheets; it opens only the spreadsheet that is open.

Slides

The tools read the tables, the positions of the elements and the text of the open presentation, to change borders, fills and fonts, or to delete elements that reach over the edge of a slide. GWS Whizard asks for access to your Google Slides presentations; it opens only the presentation that is open.

What it keeps

No settings and no copies of your files: what a tool changes stays in the file you work in. The one exception is the email “Send invoice to Corpay” sends, with the document as a PDF: it stays in your Gmail Trash until Gmail deletes it after 30 days, or in your Sent mail if it could not be moved to the Trash. If sending failed, the unsent email can stay in your Drafts. Like the other add-ons, it writes technical logs; see How long data is kept.

What it sends

The email to Corpay, and nothing else. Its other requests go only to Google’s own services, such as Docs, Sheets, Slides, Drive and Gmail.

Bloodgethound

Bloodgethound adds Loop UX’s project tools to Google Sheets: the fieldwork Schedule, invoice formatting, row tools and custom formulas. It works in the spreadsheet that is open, also reads day templates from a central Loop UX spreadsheet, and sends nothing from the open spreadsheet outside Google.

Reads
The open spreadsheet: its schedule, invoice rows, tab names and file name. Day templates from a central Loop UX spreadsheet.
Keeps
The schedule, in the spreadsheet itself. Place searches, for a few hours.
Sends
Place names you search, to Google. The Holiday Calendar shows a page from officeholidays.com.

The Schedule

“Open Schedule” shows a calendar of fieldwork sessions. It reads and writes the schedule in the spreadsheet: market, time zone, date, start, length, type and session. Session is a free-text field and can contain a participant’s code or name. The window can copy the sessions, with that text, for Loop UX’s planning spreadsheet, from which Scheduler books them in Zoom and Google Calendar with that text in their titles. In the planning spreadsheet, Loop UX’s staff give each session a code, not a participant’s name. The window also reads day templates, which hold no personal data, from a central Loop UX spreadsheet.

The add-on also keeps the tabs named Schedule up to date. These show the session names and can be shared with the project’s client.

The search for a country or city sends the text you type to Google’s geocoder. The results are kept for a few hours.

Other tools

  • “Format Invoice sheet”, “Insert row(s) below” and “Unhide all rows” read and change rows of the open sheet.
  • “Enable” lets the open spreadsheet import data from a central Loop UX spreadsheet. It reads and changes no cell.
  • The custom formulas read the names of the tabs, the file name and a cell’s formula.
  • Automatic timestamps: in some sheets, an edit inside a set range makes the add-on write the current date and time into a cell. It does not read what was edited or note who edited.
  • “Holiday Calendar” shows officeholidays.com in a side panel.

What it keeps

  • In the spreadsheet: the schedule’s rows, the Schedule tabs and the timestamps. They stay as long as the spreadsheet; see How long data is kept.
  • With the spreadsheet: technical settings of the Schedule, with no session text.
  • For you: the size of the Schedule window, and 4 settings of the window in your browser, none of them with session text.
  • Place search results, for a few hours.

What it sends

Place names go to Google’s geocoder. The add-on sends nothing from the spreadsheet to anyone else.

The Schedule window loads nothing from other sites. The Holiday Calendar shows a page from officeholidays.com; as with any website, it sees your IP address and your browser’s details. The officeholidays.com page also loads content from other companies, such as advertising and analytics services, which see the same details. Those companies and officeholidays.com may set their own cookies. None of them gets anything from the spreadsheet.

Adobe-wan Kenobi

Adobe-wan Kenobi (PDF Whizard) splits and merges PDFs, turns images into PDFs and turns SVGs into Google Drawings, from Google Drive. The files you select go to a service that Loop UX runs on Google Cloud in the EU, which works on them and keeps no copy.

Reads
The files you select in Drive, and whether they are labeled confidential.
Keeps
The new files, in Drive. No copy on the service.
Sends
The selected files, to Loop UX’s own service on Google Cloud in the EU.

What it reads

When you select files in Drive, the add-on reads their IDs, names and types, to show what it can do with them. When you click a button, it reads the full content of each selected PDF, image or SVG, the folder it is in and whether it is labeled confidential. It reads no other file, and it does not use your name or email address.

What it changes

It saves the result in the folder of the original: the 2 parts of a split PDF beside it, a Google Drawing beside each SVG, and a merged PDF in the folder of the first file in the list. If the original is in no folder you can open, the result goes to your My Drive. A file made from a file that is labeled confidential gets that label too, when the add-on can read and set the label. If the box for it is ticked, the originals that you can delete go to Drive’s trash, where you can restore them for up to 30 days.

The service on Google Cloud

The files’ content goes, over HTTPS, to a service Loop UX runs on Google Cloud in the EU. For a merge, or an image turned into a PDF, so do the files’ names and the name of the result; for an SVG, its file name. Apart from the details of the task, such as the page to split after, the service receives nothing else: not your name or email address, and no access to your Google account. It works on the files only while the request runs and keeps no copy.

What it keeps

The new files, in Drive. The add-on does not delete them; Loop UX keeps them under the rules in How long data is kept. The add-on itself stores nothing, apart from the technical logs described there.

What it sends

Only the requests to that service. Nothing goes to Adobe or to any company other than Google.

Scheduler

Scheduler brings Zoom into Google Calendar, Google Drive and Loop UX’s planning spreadsheet: pick a Zoom host for an event, import a Zoom transcript as a Google Doc, and turn planned sessions into Zoom meetings and calendar invitations. It works through Loop UX’s Zoom account, so meeting titles, times and hosts go to Zoom.

Reads
The events you add Zoom to, the planning spreadsheet, events in the calendars it is set up to read, Loop UX’s Zoom users, meetings and recordings, and the transcript you import.
Keeps
Your meeting details, until 30 days after the event, with a few exceptions. Events in 2 shared Loop UX calendars, until 60 days after they end, or longer while a booking uses them. The Zoom meetings it creates, which it does not delete when the event ends. Transcripts, as Google Docs.
Sends
Meeting titles, times and hosts, to Zoom.

Google Calendar

Under “Add video conferencing” you pick the Zoom host for the event. Scheduler loads the event from Google Calendar and uses its title, start, end and time zone; it works only with single events, not with a series. It does not use the event’s guests or its description, and sends neither to Zoom. It creates a Zoom meeting for that host in Loop UX’s Zoom account, and adds the join link, the meeting ID, the passcode and the host’s email address to the event, where the event’s guests see them.

After that it works in the background, as you: when you change the event’s title or time, it changes the Zoom meeting, and when you delete an event that you organize, it deletes the Zoom meeting. For that it reads the events it added Zoom to again when your calendar changes, and from time to time.

Google Drive: transcripts

The side panel lists a Zoom host’s cloud recordings of the past month that have a transcript, by topic and start time. It also reads the names and email addresses of the users of Loop UX’s Zoom account.

Importing downloads the transcript from Zoom, with the speakers’ names and everything that was said, and writes it into a new Google Doc in the folder you selected, or into a new tab of the document you selected.

The planning spreadsheet

In Loop UX’s planning spreadsheet, Scheduler turns rows into Zoom meetings or webinars and into events in a shared Loop UX calendar. A row holds the host, the project, the market and session, the time, the guests’ email addresses and whether to add Loop UX’s standard text for observers. Google Calendar emails the guests their invitations, and the guests of an event can see each other’s email addresses. Zoom gets a title made of the project number, the market and the row’s session text, or the group’s name when sessions share a link. Loop UX’s staff write a code there, not a participant’s name. Zoom also gets the time, the meeting’s settings and the email addresses of the meeting’s Loop UX hosts. It does not get the guests or the invitation’s text. The first time you use it, Scheduler adds the shared calendar to your Google Calendar list.

Scheduler keeps the spreadsheet, the calendar and Zoom in step in the background, without anyone opening it. Once a day it also deletes past events from that calendar and from a second shared Loop UX calendar, used for recruitment, as “What it keeps” says. For its overview of meetings, it reads the events in a range of dates in the calendars it is set up to read, which can include colleagues’ calendars, and the meetings booked in Loop UX’s Zoom account. The overview shows which project each host is booked for on each day, not the events’ titles or guests.

What it keeps

  • For you, for each event with Zoom: the event’s and the meeting’s details. This is deleted 30 days after the event ends, also when you removed Zoom from the event or Scheduler can no longer find it, or at once, with the Zoom meeting, when you delete an event that you organize before then. For an event you never save, Scheduler deletes the Zoom meeting and the record after 1 day; if Zoom did not confirm that meeting as expected, Scheduler keeps the meeting and deletes the record within 40 days after it was made. If you remove Zoom from an event and then add Zoom from another host, a short note of the first meeting stays for 30 days. In a few cases Scheduler does not delete the record by itself, for example when Zoom did not confirm a meeting that Scheduler asked it to create, when the event became a repeating series, or when Scheduler cannot tell when the event ends.
  • For you: the Zoom host you picked last, until you pick another, and notes of your recent imports, with the document and, for a new tab, its title. A later import deletes the notes that are more than a few hours old.
  • For a short time: the names and email addresses of Loop UX’s Zoom users and, for you, the list of a host’s recordings.
  • In the planning spreadsheet: each booking with its host, its guests’ email addresses, its title and links. They are removed after the session; a canceled session that shares its Zoom link with sessions that took place stays until 60 days after the last of them, and a booking or Zoom link that cannot be checked stays until someone settles it. The event in the shared calendar, with its title and its guests’ email addresses, stays until 60 days after the session, or longer while a booking still uses it, such as a later session that shares its Zoom link. Then Scheduler deletes the event and does not email its guests about it. If the shared calendar organized the event, Google also removes it from the calendars of guests who use Google Calendar, Loop UX staff included; guests who use another calendar app keep their copy. Scheduler does not delete the Zoom meeting, with its title, when the session ends: it deletes a Zoom meeting only when every session that uses it is canceled. The spreadsheet also holds the Zoom users’ names and email addresses, and a short list of Scheduler’s latest actions and errors, where newer entries replace older ones.
  • In a second shared Loop UX calendar, used for recruitment: Scheduler deletes its events 60 days after they end, unless a booking in the planning spreadsheet still uses them. It does not email their guests. If that calendar organized an event, Google also removes it from the calendars of guests who use Google Calendar, Loop UX staff included; guests who use another calendar app keep their copy.
  • The transcripts you import, as Google Docs. The add-on does not delete them; Loop UX keeps them under the rules in How long data is kept.

What it sends

Requests to Zoom (zoom.us), over HTTPS, through Loop UX’s Zoom account. Zoom gets each meeting’s title, time, length, time zone, host and settings, the email addresses of its other hosts, and Scheduler’s requests for users, meetings, settings, recordings and transcripts. It does not get an event’s guests, the text of its invitation, or any file from Drive.

peazySign

peazySign sends the tab you have open in Google Docs to BoldSign, an e-signature service, as a contract. In Google Drive, it can also save completed BoldSign documents, including responses to Bulk Links, as signed PDFs in a folder you choose.

Reads
The open document and the tab you send, the people in Loop UX’s directory, and the signers of documents you sent with the tag “Contract”. For Drive sync: the folder you choose, and the details and signed PDF of completed BoldSign documents, which can include the sender’s and signers’ names and email addresses.
Keeps
Your connections to BoldSign and Google, folder rules and settings, service records for the times its section gives, consultant suggestions for a short time and a list of accounts left out of the signer lists. No copy of a contract or of a signed PDF.
Sends
The contract PDF and signer and CC details, to BoldSign. Drive sync saves signed PDFs in the Drive folder you choose.

What it reads

You connect your BoldSign user on BoldSign’s sign-in page, so the add-on never sees your password. It reads the name and email address of the BoldSign account you connected, to show which account that is.

To let you pick the colleague who signs for Loop UX and, if you want, a colleague in CC, the add-on reads the people in Loop UX’s Google Workspace directory. Google returns each person’s profile as colleagues see it; the add-on uses the name, the email address and the profile photo, and keeps none of it except the email addresses of the accounts left out of the lists (see “What it keeps”).

When you click “Send for signature”, the add-on reads the open document, checks the text of the tab that is open and makes a PDF of that tab only. It reads the tab’s title and the document’s name for the project number. You type the name and email address of the consultant who signs. In Docs, the add-on uses its access to your Google Drive and your Google Docs documents only to check the tab, prepare it (see “What it changes”) and make the PDF. It opens no other file and changes no other document. In Drive, it reads the name and type of the item you select, and uses its access to your Drive only to give the Drive sync service access to the folder you choose.

“Check status” asks BoldSign for the contract’s status and the signer it is waiting for. To suggest a consultant while you type, the add-on requests from BoldSign the documents your BoldSign user sent recently with the tag “Contract”, which peazySign gives every contract it sends, and uses only those with the tag: their signers’ names and email addresses.

For Drive sync, you choose a folder in My Drive or a shared drive and connect your Google and BoldSign accounts for the imports. The service reads the folder’s name, identifier, type and labels, and whether you may add files and change labels there. It does not read the other files in the folder.

When a document in Loop UX’s BoldSign account is completed, including a response to a Bulk Link, BoldSign notifies the Drive sync service. The notice and the document’s details can contain the sender’s and signers’ names and email addresses. The service uses the title, original file names and tags to see whether a rule matches, and the signers’ names and email addresses to name the file where the folder asks for it. A rule saves every later completed document that matches it and that its owner’s BoldSign user can read. Imports under a rule use the connected accounts of the person who owns the rule. The service keeps neither the notice nor the document’s details; “What it keeps” lists the records it keeps.

What it changes

Just before it makes the PDF, the add-on removes the text highlighting from the open tab and deletes the drawings and images in that tab that sit in the text, beside it or in front of it, which the templates use for notes to the people who fill them in. An image set behind the text, such as the logo, stays. It does this in the document itself, even if the sending then fails; the version history still has them. The sending step changes nothing else in the document and does not save the PDF in Drive.

While a folder has an active import rule, Drive sync marks it with a peazySign label where it can. People who can see the folder’s labels can see that, but not which documents were imported. A folder without the label can still have an active rule.

What it keeps

  • For you: your connection to BoldSign, with the name and email address of the BoldSign account you connected. It stays until you disconnect, or until the add-on next finds that BoldSign has ended it.
  • While you connect an account: short-lived sign-in data, with your Google account’s identifier, its email address and the service you connect. It is used once and deleted after use or after it expires.
  • For a short time, and until your next send at the latest: the names and email addresses of the signers of the documents with the tag “Contract” that your BoldSign user sent recently, other than you and the colleagues the add-on finds in Loop UX’s directory, for the suggestions. Only you get them.
  • For everyone: the email addresses of Loop UX accounts that are left out of the signer and CC lists, such as accounts that are not people, until the list is updated.
  • For the Docs sending step: no copy of the contract or its PDF and no list of the contracts you sent.

Time Loop

Time Loop is Loop UX’s time tracking web app, which staff and the freelancers Loop UX invites open from the Google app launcher. It keeps the hours, time off and project budgets people record, in Loop UX’s Google Cloud in the EU. For staff in Denmark it is the record of daily working time that Danish law asks Loop UX to keep.

Reads
Your Google sign-in and directory photo; in your browser, only if you allow it, your calendar and the names and times of files you edit.
Keeps
Your entries, time off, schedule and settings, with a history of every change, for 5 years.
Sends
A line you write in Quick log, to Gemini on Google Cloud. Nothing to companies other than Google.

What it records

Time Loop is Loop UX’s record of working time and time off, and of the hours on each project. For each entry: the day, start and end, project, task, a note, whether it was travel, whether it was planned and confirmed, and a history of every change: who made it, when, what changed and, for older entries and for corrections by an admin, why. It also records your time off with its type and any approval, your work schedule and expected hours, your office and lunch days at the Copenhagen office, your working hours, your notification choices and your other settings. Admins and managers record some of this for you, such as your schedule. When an admin corrects one of your entries, Time Loop shows you the correction and its reason.

What it reads from Google

  • Your Google account’s email address and name, when you sign in with Google.
  • Your profile photo from Loop UX’s Google Workspace directory, to show beside your name. Time Loop copies it every night and shows it from its own address. When you leave Loop UX, it is removed.
  • Your Google Calendar, only if you allow it, read in your browser: your meetings and out-of-office time, to show beside your entries and to suggest entries; the working location you set (office or home), for lunch at the office; and, only if you show them, the events of other calendars in your Google Calendar list, which count as time you were not working. Time Loop keeps only office or home for each day and, when you link a meeting to an entry, the meeting’s ID. It never changes your calendar, and its server keeps no access to your Google account.
  • Your Google Docs, Sheets and Slides activity, only if you turn it on: the names of the files you edited and when, to suggest entries. Never their contents.
  • The Time Loop Chrome extension, if you turn it on, gives Time Loop blocks of work from your browsing; see its section of the Chrome extensions policy.

Suggestions from your calendar, your files and the extension are shown only to you. Nothing is added to your hours until you add it.

Quick log and AI

If you use Quick log, the line you write, at most 300 characters, and the names of your projects and tasks go to Gemini on Google Cloud, in the EU. Model Armor, a Google Cloud service, screens each request first. Gemini proposes entries; nothing is added until you choose Add. Under Google Cloud’s terms, Google does not use these requests to train its models. Time Loop keeps the requests and answers for 30 days, to find faults. If you speak your line instead, Chrome turns it into text on your computer, and no audio is sent.

Time Loop marks what AI proposes: Quick log says its entries come from Gemini, and a suggestion whose project Gemini Nano picked on your computer says so. The AI only proposes entries to the person who asked. It does not rate, rank or evaluate anyone, its proposals are not shown to managers or admins, and it plays no part in Insights or in any decision about your work. Meeting titles from your calendar are not sent to any AI.

Time Loop’s suggestions and its reminders about rest and working time never decide anything about you. They propose and remind; you decide.

Working-time rules

Time Loop checks the hours you record against the working-time rules for where you work, based on the EU Working Time Directive and local law: rest between working days, a break on long days, a weekly rest, your average week and night work, and for US staff the overtime threshold. It reminds you and, for the people they look after, your manager and admins. The reminders never stop you recording the hours you really worked. If your timer stops by itself, because your computer was idle or at its time limit, the entry keeps its real start, says in its history that it stopped by itself, and opens so you can change the end.

Who sees your hours

You see your own hours. Your manager sees the hours, time off and working-time reminders of the people in their region, and admins see everyone’s, to run projects, payroll and the working-time rules. Only you and admins see that a day off was a sick day; everyone else sees “Away”. Freelancers see only their own hours and the parts of projects they work on.

How long data is kept

  • Entries, time off and their history of changes: 5 years, then they are deleted automatically. Totals per project and month, without names, can be kept longer.
  • Your profile photo: until you leave Loop UX.
  • Quick log requests and answers: 30 days.
  • Backups of Time Loop’s database: daily backups for 7 days, weekly backups for 14 weeks, and a monthly copy for 12 months that cannot be changed or deleted early. A record deleted from Time Loop can stay in a backup that long.

Your choices

In My settings you can connect or disconnect your calendar, turn suggestions from your files on or off, choose how Time Loop notifies you, and set your working hours. The extension has its own switch in Chrome. Recording your working time is part of your work at Loop UX, and for staff in Denmark the law requires it.
