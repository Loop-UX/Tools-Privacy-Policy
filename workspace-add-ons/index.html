About this policy

  • The add-ons run in Loop UX’s own Google Workspace and Google Cloud. What they read stays there, except what you send with them: emails and calendar invitations go to the people you address, and Loopy’s automatic replies go to the people who wrote.
  • 3 add-ons send data to another company: Scheduler sends meeting titles and times to Zoom, GWS Whizard’s “Send invoice to Corpay” emails a document to Corpay, and peazySign sends contracts and the signers’ names and email addresses to BoldSign, an e-signature service.
  • They are published privately in the Google Workspace Marketplace, so only people with a loop-ux.com Google account can install them. “Staff” on this page means the people with a loop-ux.com account, at Loop UX ApS or at Loop UX Inc.
  • Loop UX doesn’t sell what the add-ons handle, use it for advertising or use it to train AI models.
  • This policy covers only the add-ons. Loop UX’s policy for research participants covers the studies themselves, and the Chrome extensions have their own policy.

Each add-on has its own section, which says what it reads, what it keeps and for how long, and what it sends. Some add-ons also handle data about people who don’t work at Loop UX: People outside Loop UX says what that means for them. The shared terms at the end apply to all the add-ons. For staff, Loop UX’s internal staff notice says more about how Loop UX handles staff data in its Google Workspace.

Loopy

Loopy manages your email aliases in Gmail, sends a mail merge from Google Sheets, renames and labels files in Workspace Studio flows, and converts times for your team in Google Chat. It also answers emails to closed study addresses automatically. Apart from the emails it sends, everything it handles stays in Loop UX’s Google Workspace and Google Cloud.

Reads
Your aliases, the sheet and draft you merge, file names in a flow, your Chat space’s time zones, and emails to closed study addresses.
Keeps
A space’s members and their time zones, until the next lookup replaces them. Your Chat settings. Pinned places and names added by hand, until someone removes them.
Sends
The emails you send with it and its automatic replies. Nothing to companies other than Google.

Gmail: email aliases

Opening Loopy in Gmail reads your primary address and the aliases on your account from Loop UX’s Google Workspace directory. It reads no email. Adding or removing an alias changes the directory at once, and an alias stays there until you remove it. If the alias you ask for is taken, Loopy shows you the name and address of the colleague who has it.

Google Sheets: mail merge

Loopy reads the whole tab that is open, because every column of a row can go into that row’s email. You choose the mailbox to send from: a shared Loop UX mailbox or your own.

  • Loopy reads the subjects of the drafts in the mailbox you send from, so you can pick one.
  • From the draft you pick, it reads the subject, the text, the attachments and the inline images.
  • In “Reply (to all)” mode it looks, in the mailbox you send from, for the latest email to or from each address, and answers in that thread. The reply goes to the people on that email: the person in the row and whoever else it was sent or copied to.

“Send all” sends 1 email per row to the address in that row. If the tab has a column headed Sent, Loopy writes there the time each email went out, or the error if it failed. The emails stay in the Sent folder of the mailbox, like any other email. “Send preview” sends the first row to the mailbox you send from.

Workspace Studio: flow steps

A flow gives each step the link to a Drive file, and “Rename a file” also gets the new name. The step reads the file’s name and the name and link of its folder, never what is in the file. It renames the file or applies the Drive label that marks a file as confidential, and passes the names and links of the file and its folder on to the steps that follow. Workspace Studio shows them in the flow’s activity.

Google Chat: time zones

/tz converts a time for everyone in a space. For that, Loopy reads who is in the space, asks Loop UX’s directory about each member (it uses the name and email address) and reads the time zone of each Loop UX colleague’s primary Google Calendar: only the time zone, never an event. This covers every Loop UX colleague in the space, whether or not they use Loopy. People outside Loop UX aren’t found in the directory, so Loopy keeps nothing about them unless someone adds their name and time zone by hand.

Loopy keeps:

  • The names, email addresses and time zones of a space’s members. It uses them for a few hours; the next /tz after that replaces them. A member of the space can ask Loopy to show the names and time zones it holds for that space.
  • Your settings: 12-hour or 24-hour times, and a time zone you set while you travel. They stay until you change them.
  • The places the team pinned, and the names with a time zone that someone added by hand. Pinned places are shared by all spaces and stay until someone removes them. Names added in a space are deleted when Loopy is removed from it.

The result of /tz is posted for everyone in the space and shows regions and times, no names. Loopy’s other answers are visible only to you, apart from its welcome message when it joins a space. Loopy reads only the commands you send it; if you mention it or write to it directly, it ignores the text.

Research inbox: automatic replies

Loopy also answers automatically. It reads recent emails that were written to a study’s contact address after the study closed. Where no one at Loop UX has answered yet, it sends the beginning of the email to Google Cloud Translation to detect the language, and sends 1 reply from a shared Loop UX mailbox. For a sender who doesn’t write in English, the reply’s text, with the sender’s first name in the greeting, also goes to Cloud Translation.

Loopy keeps no copy of its own: the reply stays in the mailbox. Most people who write are research participants, and Loop UX’s policy for research participants applies to them.

What it sends

Nothing to companies other than Google. Loopy’s requests go to Google’s own services: the Workspace directory, Gmail, Calendar, Chat, Drive and Cloud Translation. The emails of a mail merge and the automatic replies go to their recipients through Gmail.

Your choices

  • Remove an alias with its trash icon in Gmail.
  • Type /tz-config refresh in a space to delete what Loopy holds about its members; the next /tz looks them up again. Removing Loopy from the space deletes it too.
  • Set your time zone yourself with /tz-config, so /tz shows that one instead of your calendar’s.

GWS Whizard

GWS Whizard adds a menu of formatting tools to Google Docs, Sheets and Slides. Each tool works on the file that is open and keeps nothing. 1 tool sends something out: “Send invoice to Corpay” emails the open document to Corpay.

Reads
The file that is open, when you run a tool.
Keeps
Nothing outside the file.
Sends
Only “Send invoice to Corpay”: the document as a PDF, by email.

Docs

The tools read the text of the open document and its file name, and change the document in place.

  • 2 tools format transcripts exported from transcription services: they ask you for the speakers’ names and write them into the document. Nothing goes to those services.
  • “Link timecodes” asks for the link to a recording in Drive and uses it only to build the links.
  • The other tools change formatting only, or delete drawings and images from the open tab.

Send invoice to Corpay

This tool turns the open document into a PDF and emails it from your Gmail account to Corpay, the provider Loop UX pays invoices through. Corpay receives the PDF and the document’s file name. The PDF isn’t saved in Drive.

Afterwards the tool permanently deletes the email it sent from your Sent mail. Nothing else in your mail is touched.

This tool is why GWS Whizard asks for access to your Google Drive and your Gmail in Docs. No other tool uses either. Corpay handles the invoice on its own responsibility, under its privacy notice.

Sheets

The tools read the cells you selected or, for some, the whole sheet or every tab, and change formatting, notes and checkboxes there. “Copy content to note” puts each cell’s text into its note.

Slides

The tools read the tables, the positions of the elements and the text of the open presentation, to change borders, fills and fonts, or to delete elements that reach over the edge of a slide.

What it keeps

Nothing outside the file you work in: no settings, no copies and no history.

What it sends

The email to Corpay, and nothing else. The tools make no requests to other services.

Bloodgethound

Bloodgethound adds Loop UX’s project tools to Google Sheets: the fieldwork Schedule, invoice formatting, row tools and custom formulas. It works in the spreadsheet that is open and sends nothing in it outside Google.

Reads
The open spreadsheet: its schedule, invoice rows, tab names and file name.
Keeps
The schedule, in the spreadsheet itself. Place searches, for a few hours.
Sends
Place names you search, to Google. Its windows load files from Cloudflare and Tailwind Labs, and a page from officeholidays.com.

The Schedule

“Open Schedule” shows a calendar of fieldwork sessions. It reads and writes the spreadsheet’s Schedules table: market, time zone, date, start, length, type and session. Session is a free-text field and can contain a participant’s code or name.

The add-on also keeps the tabs named Schedule up to date. These show the session names and can be shared with the project’s client.

The search for a country or city sends the text you type to Google’s geocoder. The results are kept for a few hours, for everyone who searches the same text.

Other tools

  • “Format Invoice sheet”, “Insert row(s) below” and “Unhide all rows” read and change rows of the open sheet.
  • The custom formulas read the names of the tabs, the file name and a cell’s formula.
  • Automatic timestamps: in some sheets the add-on writes the date of your last edit into a cell. It doesn’t read what you edited.
  • “Holiday Calendar” shows officeholidays.com in a side panel.

What it keeps

  • In the spreadsheet: the schedule’s rows, the Schedule tabs and the timestamps. They stay as long as the spreadsheet; see How long data is kept.
  • With the spreadsheet: technical settings of the Schedule, with no session text.
  • For you: the size of the Schedule window, and 3 display preferences in your browser.
  • Place search results, for a few hours.

What it sends

Place names go to Google’s geocoder. Nothing in the spreadsheet goes to anyone else.

When the Schedule window opens, your browser fetches some of its files from Cloudflare, Tailwind Labs and Google Fonts, and the Holiday Calendar shows a page from officeholidays.com. As with any website, they see your IP address and your browser’s details, and officeholidays.com may set its own cookies. They get nothing from the spreadsheet.

Adobe-wan Kenobi

Adobe-wan Kenobi (PDF Whizard) splits and merges PDFs, turns images into PDFs and turns SVGs into Google Drawings, from Google Drive. The files you select go to a service that Loop UX runs on Google Cloud in the EU, which works on them and keeps nothing.

Reads
The files you select in Drive, and whether they are labeled confidential.
Keeps
The new files, in Drive. Nothing on the service.
Sends
The selected files, to Loop UX’s own service on Google Cloud in the EU.

What it reads

When you select files in Drive, the add-on reads their IDs, names and types, to show what it can do with them. When you click a button, it reads the full content of each selected PDF, image or SVG, the folder it is in and whether it is labeled confidential. It reads nothing you didn’t select, and it doesn’t use your name or email address.

What it changes

It saves the result next to the original: the merged PDF, the 2 parts of a split PDF, or a Google Drawing beside each SVG. A file made from a file that is labeled confidential gets that label too. If the box for it is ticked, the originals go to Drive’s trash, where you can restore them for 30 days.

The service on Google Cloud

The files’ content goes, over HTTPS, to a service Loop UX runs on Google Cloud in the EU. For a merge or an SVG, so do the files’ names and the name you gave the result. The service receives nothing else: no name, no email address and no access to your Google account. It works on the files only while the request runs and keeps no copy.

What it keeps

The new files, in Drive, until someone deletes them. The add-on itself keeps nothing.

What it sends

Only the requests to that service. Nothing goes to Adobe or to any other company.

Scheduler

Scheduler brings Zoom into Google Calendar and Google Drive: pick a Zoom host for an event, and import a Zoom transcript as a Google Doc. It works through Loop UX’s Zoom account, so meeting titles and times go to Zoom.

Reads
The events you add Zoom to, the planning spreadsheet and the calendar events it lists, Loop UX’s Zoom users and recordings, and the transcript you import.
Keeps
Each meeting’s details, until 30 days after the event or until the Zoom meeting is deleted. Transcripts, as Google Docs.
Sends
Meeting titles, times and hosts, to Zoom.

Google Calendar

Under “Add video conferencing” you pick a named Zoom host, or “Zoom Meeting” for your own Zoom user. Scheduler loads the event from Google Calendar and uses its title, start, end, time zone and repeat rule. It doesn’t use the event’s guests or its description, and sends neither to Zoom. It creates a Zoom meeting for that host in Loop UX’s Zoom account, and adds the join link, the meeting ID, the passcode and the host’s email address to the event, where the event’s guests see them.

After that it works in the background, as you. When you change the event’s title or time, it changes the Zoom meeting, and when you delete an event that you organize, it deletes the Zoom meeting. For that it reads the events it added Zoom to again when your calendar changes.

Google Drive: transcripts

The side panel lists the active users of Loop UX’s Zoom account, by name and email address. For the host you pick, it lists the cloud recordings of the past month that have a transcript, by topic and start time. Staff who use Scheduler can list and import transcripts of recordings in Loop UX’s Zoom account, including meetings hosted by colleagues.

Importing downloads the transcript from Zoom, with the speakers’ names and everything that was said, and writes it into a new Google Doc in the folder you selected, or into a new tab of the document you selected.

The planning spreadsheet

In Loop UX’s planning spreadsheet, Scheduler turns rows into Zoom meetings or webinars and into events in a shared Loop UX calendar. A row holds the host, the project, the market and session, a description, the time and the guests’ email addresses. Google Calendar emails the guests their invitations. Zoom gets the topic, the time, the passcode and, as alternative hosts, the email addresses of Loop UX’s other licensed Zoom users. It never gets the guests or the description. The first time you use it, Scheduler adds the shared calendar to your Google Calendar list.

Scheduler keeps the spreadsheet, the calendar and Zoom in step automatically, without anyone opening it. For its overview of meetings it reads the events, in the overview’s range of dates, of the calendars that are shared with it, and looks up at Zoom the meeting IDs it finds in them.

What it keeps

  • For you, for each event with Zoom: the event’s and the meeting’s details, including the join link and the passcode. This is deleted 30 days after the event ends or when the Zoom meeting is deleted. If you remove Zoom from an event, or the event can no longer be found, the record stays until the Zoom meeting is deleted.
  • For you: the Zoom host you picked last, and a note of each import (the document’s ID and the tab’s title) until a later import clears it.
  • For a short time: the names and email addresses of Loop UX’s Zoom users and, for you, the list of a host’s recordings.
  • In the planning spreadsheet: each booking with its host, its guests’ email addresses, its title, description and links, in its row and in a working record. Both are removed after the session; a booking that can’t be checked stays until someone settles it. Also the Zoom users’ names and email addresses.
  • The transcripts you import, as Google Docs. They are kept as long as the study they belong to; see How long data is kept.

What it sends

Requests to Zoom (zoom.us), over HTTPS, through Loop UX’s Zoom account. Zoom gets each meeting’s title, time, length, time zone and host, and the requests for the list of users, each host’s meetings, settings and recordings, and a transcript. It never gets an event’s guests, the text of its description or anything from Drive.

peazySign

The peazySign add-on sends the tab you have open in Google Docs to BoldSign, an e-signature service, as a contract: a consultant signs first, a colleague at Loop UX second. It sends as your own BoldSign user, so the contract and the signers’ names and email addresses go to BoldSign.

Reads
The tab you send, the people in Loop UX’s directory with their name, email address and profile photo, and the status of a contract you sent.
Keeps
Your connection to BoldSign, until you disconnect or BoldSign ends it, and a list of the accounts left out of the signer and CC lists. No contracts: they are in Loop UX’s BoldSign account, which deletes a contract 60 days after it is signed by everyone, declined, canceled by the sender or expired.
Sends
The contract as a PDF, the signers’ names and email addresses and a CC address, to BoldSign.

What it reads

You connect your own BoldSign user. Connect the BoldSign user Loop UX invited you to. A window opens BoldSign’s sign-in page, so the add-on never sees your password. It then asks BoldSign for the email address and name of the account you connected, to show in the side panel which account that is. The connection lasts until you disconnect or BoldSign ends it.

Once you are connected, opening the add-on in Google Docs asks Loop UX’s Google Workspace directory for the people in it, so you can pick the colleague who signs for Loop UX and, if you want, a colleague in CC. Google answers with each person’s profile as colleagues see it. The add-on uses the name, the email address and the profile photo, and keeps none of it. It uses your own email address to preselect you as the signer for Loop UX. It shows your own photo next to the connected account when that account has your address.

When you click “Send for signature”, the add-on reads the tab that is open and makes a PDF of it. It reads the tab’s title and the document’s name to find the project number at their start, and asks you for the number if there is none. You type the name and email address of the consultant who signs. The add-on asks for read access to your Google Drive because making the PDF needs it. It opens no other file.

“Check status” asks BoldSign about the contract you just sent: its status and the signer it is waiting for. The add-on reads no other document in your BoldSign account.

What it changes

Just before it makes the PDF, the add-on removes the text highlighting from the open tab: its body, header, footer and footnotes. It does this in the document itself, not only in the PDF. The highlighting stays removed if the sending then fails. The add-on changes nothing else in the document, and the PDF isn’t saved in Drive.

What it keeps

  • For you: your connection to BoldSign, meaning the tokens that let the add-on send contracts and check their status as you, and the email address and name of the BoldSign account you connected. They stay until you click “Disconnect BoldSign”, which also asks BoldSign to end the connection, or until the add-on finds that BoldSign no longer accepts the connection.
  • For up to 1 hour after the add-on shows you “Connect BoldSign”: a one-time code for the sign-in.
  • For everyone: the email addresses of Loop UX accounts that are left out of the signer and CC lists, such as accounts that are not people. An admin sets which accounts and organizational units are left out. When an admin uses the add-on, it looks up the units’ members in the directory, at most once an hour, and keeps that list until the next look-up.
  • Nothing else: no copy of the contract or the PDF and no list of the contracts you sent.

The contract, the signers’ names and email addresses and BoldSign’s record of the signing are in Loop UX’s BoldSign account; see How long data is kept. That record holds the name, email address, IP address and device of everyone who takes a step with the contract, how each signer signed, and the time of each step.

What it sends

Requests to BoldSign (boldsign.com), over HTTPS, as your own BoldSign user. With a contract, BoldSign gets:

  • the open tab as a PDF;
  • the consultant’s name and email address, and the name and email address of the colleague who signs for Loop UX;
  • the email address of the colleague in CC, if you picked one;
  • a title made of the project number and the consultant’s name, and a fixed sentence that asks the signers to review and sign;
  • where the signature, date and location fields go on the page;
  • the project number, the IDs of the Google document and its tab, a fixed label for this add-on and your choice for the signing location, as a reference.

BoldSign then emails the signers a link to the contract: the consultant first, then the colleague who signs for Loop UX. BoldSign also emails the colleague in CC, who can view and download the contract but not sign it.

BoldSign also gets your requests to connect, to check a contract’s status and to disconnect. It gets no other file from your Drive, no one from the directory but the people you picked, and no access to your Google account. The page that confirms the connection loads a font and the add-on’s icon from Google, and shows the name and email address of the account you connected.

When you open BoldSign’s pages, to sign in or to sign a contract, Syncfusion, Inc., the company that runs BoldSign, also handles technical data about your device and browser on its own responsibility, to prevent fraud; see Who gets the data.

How long data is kept

The add-on keeps your connection to BoldSign until you disconnect or BoldSign ends it, and the one-time sign-in code for up to 1 hour. It keeps the list of left-out accounts until an admin changes it or the next look-up replaces it.

The contract, the signers’ names and email addresses and the record of the signing are in Loop UX’s BoldSign account. Loop UX has set that account to delete a contract 60 days after it is signed by everyone, declined, canceled by the sender or expired. A contract that is still waiting for a signature stays in BoldSign until then. BoldSign says it keeps backups of its databases for 30 days.

BoldSign does not say that it deletes its record of the signing together with the contract. Its terms allow it to keep such records for as long as reasonably necessary and as the law requires. For what it keeps for its own legal reasons, Syncfusion acts on its own responsibility.

Loop UX keeps the signed contract in its own Google Drive for up to 5 years.

BoldSign emails the signed contract to everyone who signed and to the colleague in CC. Those copies stay with the people who got them.

People outside Loop UX

Some add-ons handle data about people who don’t work at Loop UX. If you are one of them, this is what happens with it.

If you take part in a study

Loop UX’s staff may:

  • email you from a spreadsheet that holds your name, your email address and details of your session (Loopy);
  • plan your session under a code or your name (Bloodgethound), share that schedule with the study’s client, and invite you to the session by Google Calendar (Scheduler);
  • turn the Zoom transcript of a recorded session, with what you said, into a Google Doc (Scheduler). What you said can include sensitive topics.

If you write to a study’s address after the study has closed, you get 1 automatic reply. To write it in your language, the beginning of your email goes to Google Cloud Translation and, if you don’t write in English, so does your first name, in the greeting of the reply.

Loop UX’s policy for research participants says what your data is used for, how long it is kept and how to withdraw.

If you work with Loop UX

This is for clients, freelancers and guests. If a colleague at Loop UX invites you to a session, your email address is in Loop UX’s planning spreadsheet and in the Google Calendar invitation. If a Zoom meeting you joined was recorded, your name and what you said can be in its transcript, in a Google Doc at Loop UX. If Loop UX pays your invoice through Corpay, the invoice is emailed to Corpay.

If a colleague at Loop UX sends you a contract to sign, the contract, your name and your email address go to BoldSign, the e-signature service Loop UX uses; the peazySign section lists what it gets. BoldSign emails you a link to the contract. It keeps a record of the signing, with your name, your email address, your IP address, the device you use, how you signed and the time of each step. The contract and that record are in Loop UX’s BoldSign account, which BoldSign stores in the Netherlands. When you open BoldSign’s pages, Syncfusion, Inc., the company that runs BoldSign, also handles technical data about your device and browser on its own responsibility, to prevent fraud. BoldSign’s privacy notice says how.

Loop UX has set its BoldSign account to delete a contract 60 days after it is signed by everyone, declined, canceled by the sender or expired. BoldSign emails the signed contract to everyone who signed and to the colleague at Loop UX in CC, if there is one. Those copies stay with the people who got them. BoldSign may keep its record of the signing longer than the contract: its terms allow that for as long as reasonably necessary and as the law requires. Loop UX keeps the signed contract in its own Google Drive for up to 5 years; see How long data is kept.

Where your data comes from

Loop UX gets your details from you, from the colleague at Loop UX who works with you, or from the recruiter or the client of the study you take part in. You don’t have to give Loop UX this data. Without your email address Loop UX can’t invite you or send you a contract to sign, and without your invoice it can’t pay you.

Your rights

You can ask Loop UX for a copy of your data, to correct or erase it, or to limit its use. Where the basis is your consent, you can withdraw it at any time; what was done before stays lawful. You can then also ask for your data in a common file format. Where the basis is Loop UX’s legitimate interest, you can object; see Your right to object. The bases, how long data is kept and how to complain are in the shared terms. Write to data@loop-ux.com.

Shared terms

These terms apply to all the add-ons.

Who is responsible

Loop UX ApS, Bryghuspladsen 8, 3. sal, 1473 København K, Denmark, CVR no. 37 82 07 76, makes the add-ons and is the data controller for what they handle, under the GDPR. Write to data@loop-ux.com with any question about this policy.

Purpose and legal basis

The add-ons take routine steps out of Loop UX’s project work: email aliases, mailings, schedules, video meetings, transcripts, invoices, contracts and the formatting of files.

  • Staff: Loop UX’s legitimate interest in doing this work with tools of its own, inside its own Google Workspace (GDPR Article 6(1)(f)).
  • Study participants: your consent to the study (Article 6(1)(a), and Article 9(2)(a) for sensitive data).
  • Client contacts and other people in meetings, invitations and emails: Loop UX’s legitimate interest in planning and documenting its projects (Article 6(1)(f)).
  • People who write to a closed study’s address: Loop UX’s legitimate interest in answering you (Article 6(1)(f)).
  • People whose invoices Loop UX pays: the contract with you or with the company you work for (Article 6(1)(b)), and Danish bookkeeping law for keeping the invoice (Article 6(1)(c)).
  • People who sign a contract with Loop UX: the contract with you, and the steps to enter into it (Article 6(1)(b)). If you sign for a company: Loop UX’s legitimate interest in entering into the contract with that company (Article 6(1)(f)). For the record of the signing and for keeping the signed contract: Loop UX’s legitimate interest in being able to show who signed, when, and what was agreed (Article 6(1)(f)), and Danish bookkeeping law where it requires Loop UX to keep the contract (Article 6(1)(c)).

The add-ons’ logs exist to find faults. The add-ons make no automated decisions about anyone.

Who gets the data

How long data is kept

Each section says how long an add-on keeps what it stores itself. What the add-ons create in Google Workspace are ordinary files, emails and calendar events, and Loop UX keeps them as long as the project or contract they belong to:

  • Study material, such as mailing lists, schedules and transcripts: no longer than 5 years after it was last used, as the policy for research participants says.
  • Invoices sent to Corpay: Corpay keeps them under its own rules. The invoice document itself stays in Loop UX’s Drive with its bookkeeping records, for 5 years after the end of the financial year, as Danish bookkeeping law requires.
  • Contracts sent for signature: peazySign keeps no copy. Loop UX’s BoldSign account deletes a contract 60 days after it is signed by everyone, declined, canceled by the sender or expired. BoldSign may keep its record of the signing longer, as its terms allow. Loop UX keeps the signed contract in its own Google Drive for up to 5 years; see peazySign.

The add-ons write technical logs to Google Cloud Logging, to find faults. Depending on the add-on, an entry can contain file or tab names and IDs, the text of a command or a search, or a name or email address involved in an error. The logs are deleted after 30 days.
